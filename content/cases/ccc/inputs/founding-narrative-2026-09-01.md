In November 2010, Roger Penrose — the mathematical physicist whose singularity theorems had helped make the Big Bang respectable science — announced that it was visible from the far side. With the Armenian mathematical physicist Vahe Gurzadyan, he reported that the microwave sky — the 13.8-billion-year-old radiation that is cosmology's primary document — contains [families of concentric circles over which the temperature varies anomalously little, at significance running as high as six sigma]{claim=CCC-C002}. On the theory the circles were meant to confirm, each family is a scar: the gravitational shockwaves of supermassive black holes colliding, over and over, in a universe that existed before ours.

The theory is Conformal Cyclic Cosmology, and Penrose had published it four years before anyone went looking for circles. Its starting point is the strangest fact about our universe's beginning: the Big Bang was, gravitationally, in a state of near-perfect order — absurdly low entropy — which is why the Second Law of thermodynamics has had somewhere to go ever since. Penrose's proposal replaces "beginning" with "crossover." In the unimaginably remote future, after the last black holes have evaporated, the universe contains almost nothing but massless radiation. To a photon, which experiences no time, an eternity of empty expansion and an initial singularity are — conformally, which is to say up to a change of scale — the same kind of place. CCC takes the identification literally: [the infinite future of one aeon is the Big Bang of the next]{claim=CCC-C009}, geometry permitting, forever.

The construction has real costs, which its own author itemizes. For the far future to forget its scale, every particle with rest mass must eventually shed it — [a "mass fade-out" that Penrose calls an admittedly unconventional hypothesis]{claim=CCC-C005}, with the electron, whose decay charge conservation forbids, as the sharpest case. The entropy bookkeeping is stranger still: [each aeon can start clean only if black holes genuinely destroy information]{claim=CCC-C006}, a position most of quantum gravity rejects, and a 2023 analysis argues the destruction would raise the cosmic ledger rather than reset it. And because CCC deletes inflation, [the burden of explaining the CMB's almost-even texture falls on the previous aeon's dark matter]{claim=CCC-C008} — which the theory says consists of [Planck-mass particles Penrose named erebons, born at the crossover and decaying, detectably, ever since]{claim=CCC-C007}.

What sets CCC apart from every other story about "before the Big Bang" is that it walked onto the record and made claims about data everyone already has. Those claims are this case.

## The circles, examined

{plate:IMG-CCC-P01}

Take the observation first, interpretation second. [Quiet rings in the microwave sky exist — every group that has searched for them finds them]{claim=CCC-C001}, and that includes the theory's fiercest critics, who reproduced the Gurzadyan–Penrose procedure down to its outputs and said so in print. The dispute begins one step later, at a question that sounds too simple to fight over: compared to what?

Significance means: unlikely under the null hypothesis. Gurzadyan and Penrose computed theirs against simulated skies of pure Gaussian randomness plus instrument noise. Within weeks of the announcement, three independent groups — in Oslo, Vancouver, and Toronto — ran the same search against skies simulated from the standard cosmological model, whose fluctuations are correlated across the sky in precisely measured ways. In those richer simulated skies, the circles appeared in the same abundance as in the real one. The Vancouver group's verdict became the episode's epitaph: Gurzadyan and Penrose had "not found evidence for pre-Big Bang phenomena, but have simply re-discovered that the CMB contains structure." [The entire six-sigma anomaly lived in the choice of null ensemble]{claim=CCC-C003} — and, remarkably, the proponents agreed about the fact while contesting the moral, insisting that a sky simulated with the standard model's power spectrum smuggles the very structure in question into the definition of chance.

The proponents did not stop there, and fairness requires saying so. Their strongest reply is the "sky-twist" test: if the concentric families are real relics of expanding spherical wavefronts, they should be circular, and [Gurzadyan and Penrose report that the signal collapses when you search for even slightly elliptical rings instead]{claim=CCC-C503} — a comparison of the sky against distorted versions of itself, with no simulations to argue over. They also note that [the circle centers clump extravagantly — 83 percent of them in three patches covering 3 percent of the searched sky]{claim=CCC-C504} — which they read as superclusters in the previous aeon. No independent group has ever run the sky-twist test. That is a real gap in the critical record, and this case logs it as one.

But the deepest cut came in 2024, from a Santa Barbara–Leiden team that went looking with the finest resolution yet and found the trapdoor under the whole method: [a handful of freak few-pixel spots in the maps inflate the local variance baseline, and the search criterion then certifies rings around them as "anomalously quiet"]{claim=CCC-C010}. Screen out the freak pixels and, at full resolution, no statistically significant circles remain.

## The Hawking points

By then the proponents had moved to a second signature. A supermassive black hole from the previous aeon does not only collide; eventually it evaporates, and CCC says its entire Hawking-radiation output funnels through the crossover into a compact hot spot. In 2020, Daniel An, Krzysztof Meissner, Paweł Nurowski and Penrose reported exactly that: [a family of raised-temperature spots of a characteristic size, present in the Planck data at 99.98 percent confidence]{claim=CCC-C004}, with the five strongest spots sitting at the same sky locations in the WMAP data — two instruments, different noise, one signal.

The published rebuttal is a small masterpiece of statistical hygiene. Dylan Jow and Douglas Scott observed that the 99.98 percent was quoted for the ring size that worked best — after the size had been chosen by looking. Correct for the family of sizes actually searched, and the significance falls to 87 percent: one sigma, weather. Two further independent searches — one with a purpose-built detector that demonstrably recovers synthetic CCC spots, one with a machine-learning pipeline and the freak-pixel screening — found nothing significant, and the second could rule out Hawking points of the claimed character outright across a stated amplitude range. What survives on the proponent side is the cross-map coincidence of the strongest spots, which no critic has specifically dismantled — and which proves the spots are in the sky rather than in the instruments, without proving they are from anywhere but this universe.

## Where the case actually lives

Step back and the pattern is unusually clean. There is no dispute about the sky: every feature the proponents report, the critics reproduce. There is no dispute about the mathematics of the theory: the crossover equations are worked out and their open problems are catalogued by sympathetic hands. The entire evidentiary contest has collapsed into statistical methodology — [which ensemble defines "expected," how to price the act of searching, and what a-posteriori pattern-finding is worth]{claim=CCC-C011}. Fourteen years in, not one analysis on either side has been preregistered. The proponents chose their statistics after exploring the maps; the critics chose theirs after reading the proponents; each new proponent argument (sky-twist, anisotropy, cross-map coincidence) arrived after the previous one was answered. This is not a symmetric sin — a null result found under the claimant's own frozen criteria carries a weight a post-hoc positive does not, and the strictest critical study did exactly that. But it explains why the same sky keeps signing affidavits for both parties.

It also points at the exit. The searches are desk work on public data with open-source tooling; what has never existed is a protocol both camps endorsed before unblinding — statistic, ensemble, mask, artifact screen, look-elsewhere correction, all frozen. And beneath that, the deficit the 2024 team named politely: CCC's predictions are qualitative. Some rings, some spots, somewhere, at some strength. Until the theory says how many, how bright, and how shaped, positive claims cannot be credited and null results cannot be fatal — the definition of a dispute that can run forever. A first step has now been taken: a 2025 Meissner–Penrose preprint proposes a relation tying each hot spot's temperature to the mass of its source cluster in the previous aeon, and reports agreement with the largest clusters we observe. It is unreviewed, its calculation checked by no one outside the program — and the how-many and how-shaped questions remain open.

## What would change things

Three developments would move this case decisively, and none is hypothetical technology. A preregistered adversarial reanalysis of the final Planck maps — including the never-independently-run sky-twist test — would settle the existing claims. The coming generation of B-mode polarization experiments bears on the framework itself: [a confirmed detection of primordial gravitational waves would evidence an inflationary era that CCC, which abolished inflation, cannot supply]{claim=CCC-C008} — the rare place where the theory is exposed to data it has not already survived. And the erebon hypothesis waits for anyone to take it literally: [Penrose has suggested the decays might already be lurking in gravitational-wave detector noise]{claim=CCC-C505}; no one has ever run the search.

The universe before this one is not a foolish idea. It is a serious idea whose observational case has, so far, failed review — brought by a great physicist, examined by competent critics, and stuck at an impasse that is methodological rather than cosmic. The sky is not going anywhere. What the case is waiting for is a protocol.
