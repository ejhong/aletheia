# Product Specification

## Working title

**Athanatos Evidence Atlas**

Subtitle:

**Contested claims, mapped to evidence and experiments.**

The title and subtitle must be configurable.

## Product thesis

Many important controversies are presented at the wrong scale.

A broad thesis is often discussed as a single yes-or-no proposition even though it depends on dozens or hundreds of claims with different evidence quality, different dependencies, and different implications.

Athanatos Evidence Atlas decomposes a controversy so a reader can see:

- which lower-level observations are credible;
- which inferential steps remain weak;
- what evidence favors each competing explanation;
- where disagreement actually resides;
- what new experiment or analysis would matter most.

## Primary audiences

### Curious reader

Wants a compelling overview and a fair explanation without reading every source.

### Serious investigator

Wants atomic claims, exact sources, methodology, dependencies, and unresolved objections.

### Domain expert

Wants to correct errors, identify missing evidence, challenge interpretations, or propose decisive tests.

### Funder

Wants to know which unresolved research project has the highest potential information gain relative to cost.

### Editor

Wants a transparent workflow for reviewing evidence proposals and assessment changes.

## Core experience

A visitor should be able to enter at any level:

1. read a magazine-quality Case overview;
2. open the exact Claim behind a consequential sentence;
3. inspect evidence supporting and undermining it;
4. open the Source and exact locator;
5. compare alternative hypotheses;
6. understand what would alter the assessment;
7. view the best next experiment;
8. see how the conclusion changed over time.

## Core Case anatomy

Every mature Case includes:

- Overview
- Current assessment
- Competing hypotheses
- Claim hierarchy
- Evidence ledger
- Source library
- Research cruxes
- Research opportunities
- Change history
- Method and provenance disclosures

## Assessment philosophy

Avoid a single central “truth percentage.”

Use explicit labels:

- Established
- Well supported
- Provisionally supported
- Mixed
- Weakly supported
- Contradicted
- Unresolved
- Presently untestable

Where useful, show ranges and uncertainty. Always distinguish local claim credibility from diagnostic value for the larger hypothesis.

## Product principles

### Progressive disclosure

Do not force every reader to confront the full graph. Start readable and allow descent into detail.

### Symmetric structure

Evidence for and against a claim receives the same data structure and visual seriousness.

### Person-independent analysis

The unit of evaluation is the Claim, not the reputation, personality, or social identity of the proponent.

### Research-oriented endings

A Case should not end only with a verdict. It should identify the unresolved crux and the best way to learn more.

### Visible revision

Trust comes partly from showing corrections and changed minds.

### AI transparency

AI involvement should be disclosed at the record level. AI output is not itself evidence.

## Phase 1 non-goals

The mockup does not need:

- real user accounts;
- production data storage;
- automated browsing;
- live model calls;
- submission moderation;
- a real citation corpus;
- advanced Bayesian calculation;
- payments or grant administration.

The purpose of Phase 1 is to validate comprehension, information architecture, visual language, and editorial flow.
