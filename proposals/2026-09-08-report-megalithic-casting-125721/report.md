I'll start with a batch of independent searches across the most decisive gaps: the Al-Ma'mun salt report (GEO-R001), independent Pumapunku lithic studies, non-proponent physical measurements on pyramid blocks, and the Aswan shafts.
# Working report — GEO-001 "Cast, Not Carved" (bounded pass, 2026-09)

## 1. Findings

**F1. The al-Maʾmūn "salt layer" report (GEO-C003, GEO-E004, GEO-R001) is not in the two accessible translations of the medieval Arabic accounts, and the proponent page that carries it gives no source.**
- *Observed.* The Geopolymer Institute page (SRC-GEOPOLYMER-INST) states that when the Caliph opened the pyramid "in 820 AD" he found the interior stone covered with a 1.5 cm layer of halite; no author, work, or edition is cited for this ([geopolymer.org, "Pyramids (3)", section on NaCl](https://www.geopolymer.org/archaeology/pyramids/pyramids-3-the-formula-the-invention-of-stone/)). The same page dismisses the sedimentary-origin explanation of the salt as "silly reasoning" without argument.
- *Observed.* Nemoy's 1939 translation of al-Suyūṭī's *Tuḥfat al-kirām* (Isis 30:1, pp. 17–37), which compiles al-Masʿūdī, al-Maqrīzī and others, gives the al-Maʾmūn entry twice (pp. 24–25 and 28–29): fire, vinegar, iron tools, catapults; a wall twenty cubits thick; a well, chambers, a covered basin of granite containing "worn out pieces of rope" or decayed remains. No salt is mentioned. Nemoy's n.7 dates the visit to 217/832 ([Nemoy 1939, archive.org PDF](https://ia800102.us.archive.org/27/items/TreatiseOnTheEgyptianPyramidsSuyuti_201801/Treatise%20on%20the%20Egyptian%20Pyramids_%20Suyuti.pdf)).
- *Observed.* Cooperson's study of the episode quotes the earlier reports preserved by al-Idrīsī (d. 1251) — Ibn Mammātī and Abū al-Ṣalt al-Andalusī — describing the breach, passages, an 8×8-cubit room and a marble basin; again no salt (pp. 204–205). He places the caliph's Egyptian stay in early 832, for forty-seven days ([Cooperson, "Early Abbasid Antiquarianism"](https://www.academia.edu/25357489/Early_Abbasid_Antiquarianism_al_Ma%CA%BFm%C5%ABn_and_the_Pyramid_of_Cheops)).
- *Inference.* GEO-C003 as worded ("accounts … report interior chamber walls coated with salt") is presently unsupported by any primary text this pass could reach; the date "c. 820" in the claim is also wrong (832). This does not prove no Arabic source says it (al-Maqrīzī's full *Khiṭaṭ* chapter and ʿAbd al-Laṭīf al-Baghdādī were not opened), but the burden now sits with the proponent side to produce the passage. GEO-E004's strength should fall from "weak" to something like "unsourced/contested".

**F2. Salt in the Queen's Chamber is real, independently documented since 1883, and now characterised in situ — with a conventional geological explanation the ledger does not yet record.**
- *Observed (via transcription).* Petrie 1883 §41 (p. 66) measures the Queen's Chamber "after allowing suitably in each part for the incrustation of salt", and §43 (pp. 69–70) notes salt exudation on the two lower laps of the niche and none on the upper three ([transcribed at khufupyramid.dk](https://khufupyramid.dk/inside-dimensions/the-queens-chamber); I could not open the scan itself — the archive.org OCR was truncated).
- *Observed.* Sessa et al., *Scientific Reports* 16:12586 (16 April 2026, ScanPyramids), sampled nine efflorescence/detached specimens from the Queen's Chamber and Thieves' Corridor; XRD found only halite, calcite and quartz; SEM shows halite whiskers (Fig. 5), Na–Cl correlation R = 0.969 (Fig. 6), no nitrates. In the King's Chamber, efflorescence is attributed to kaolinisation of the Aswan granite. They cite Klemm & Klemm 2010 that halite is the primary salt within the stratigraphic layers of Giza nummulitic limestone, "likely precipitated from evaporating marine or lagoonal waters" — i.e. sedimentary, not introduced ([Sessa et al. 2026, Abstract; "Sampling"; Figs. 5–7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-48805-8)). They also record that pyramid interiors were cleaned in the late 1990s and that salt has re-formed markedly since 2022.
- *Observed.* Legon (DE 27, 1993, p. 36 area, Fig. 1 caption) refers to salt incrustations "recently removed from the walls of the Queen's Chamber" ([Legon 1993 PDF](https://gizamedia.rc.fas.harvard.edu/documents/legon_de_27_1993.pdf)).
- *Inference.* The phenomenon behind GEO-C003/GEO-C506 is genuine but the diagnostic reading is weak: halite is reported as a native constituent of the very limestone conventionally identified as the source, and efflorescence is humidity-driven and recurrent. GEO-C506's comparison claim ("comparable natural limestone monuments do not exhibit") has no cited comparator; Sessa et al. note the same salt problem in Menkaure's chambers, which does not discriminate because proponents claim that pyramid too. The decisive comparator is *unworked Mokattam/Giza bedrock and quarry faces*, which Klemm & Klemm apparently address — that primary source should be opened. F2 also supplies a concrete instance for GEO-C594 (undocumented/partly documented cleaning affecting surface evidence).

**F3. A non-proponent paleomagnetic pilot study exists and is not in the ledger (bears on GEO-C020, GEO-C589).**
- *Observed.* Túnyi & El-hemaly, "Paleomagnetic investigation of the great Egyptian pyramids", *Europhysics News* 43/6 (2012) 28–31, DOI 10.1051/epn/2012604. Samples from 7 locations (Khufu, Khafre, adjacent quarry). Three locations (2 Khafre, 1 Khufu) show a common N–S paleodirection (Fig. 4); locations 3, 4 and 7 share demagnetisation behaviour with the quarry sample and are read as natural quarry stone; location 6 is discarded for susceptibility instability. Conclusion: "a mixture of natural and artificial limestone blocks" is the most probable reading if the concrete technique was used ([EPN PDF, pp. 29–31](https://www.europhysicsnews.org/articles/epn/pdf/2012/06/epn2012436p28.pdf)).
- *Inference.* This is the only independent physical measurement on in-situ pyramid blocks that leans toward a mixed model, and it introduces a method class absent from GEO-C589. Its weaknesses are serious: a magazine feature, not a refereed paper; n = 7 locations; the test assumes quarried blocks were rotated randomly, whereas blocks laid bedding-horizontal would keep natural inclination and only randomise declination; no natural-limestone baseline for the strength of a depositional remanence in Mokattam limestone; it tests a "Nile mud + natron" recipe (Demortier) rather than the C–S–H model of GEO-E017. Strength: weak-to-moderate support, high diagnostic *potential* if replicated at scale with blinded sampling.

**F4. Pumapunku: independent provenance and quarry-landscape evidence now has a proponent reply — and both are missing from the ledger (GEO-C021, GEO-C004, GEO-C030, GEO-E008).**
- *Observed.* Janusek, Williams, Golitko & Lémuz Aguirre, "Building Taypikala" (in *Mining and Quarrying in the Ancient Andes*, Springer 2013, pp. 65–97): pXRF on monuments at Tiwanaku, Lukurmata and Iwawe defines a core "Group 1" andesite matched to outcrops on Mount Ccapia (Fig. 4.21, 90% ellipses), accounting for most Akapana volcanic blocks, the Kalasasaya west wall, the Sun Portal and the Ponce and Suñawi monoliths; roughly carved andesite "tired stones" lie at Iwawe (Fig. 4.14) and at Ccapia's foothills; the Kaliri sandstone quarry shows exploited fissures, a flake-littered working platform, and a quarried block with rope holds en route (Figs. 4.16–4.19). Critically, the authors write that the andesite source consisted of "(as yet undocumented) quarries located on Mount Ccapia" ([academia.edu full text, pp. 81–82, 88–90](https://www.academia.edu/2970308/Building_Taypikala_Telluric_Transformations_in_the_Lithic_Production_of_Tiwanaku)).
- *Observed.* Davidovits & F. Davidovits, GPSA 1 (2020) 36–43, Part IV: proponents now hold that builders transported natural andesite *sand* from Cerro Khapia (Ccapia), stored at Iwawe (Isbell & Burkholder 2002, Stratum V), and "did not use the many quadrangular volcanic blocks", i.e. the piedras cansadas ([geopolymer.org abstract](https://www.geopolymer.org/library/gpsa/2020-davidovits-andesite-sand/)).
- *Inference.* The Ccapia geochemical match is exactly the case GEO-C030 says cannot discriminate — and the proponents have adopted that reading. What *does* discriminate is (a) the carved ashlars abandoned en route, which the proponent model leaves unexplained, and (b) the Iwawe "andesite sand" deposit, which is a checkable stratigraphic claim against the excavators' own description. The absence of documented andesite quarry faces on Ccapia (as of 2013) is a real gap in the orthodox account and should be recorded as such, not as evidence for casting. GEO-E008 (Ponce 1971, sandstone) is no longer the only counterweight; the andesite provenance work is the relevant one.

**F5. Aswan: fire-setting and boulder extraction are documented complements to pounding (GEO-C001, GEO-C002, GEO-C511, GEO-C515/537 analogues).**
- *Observed.* Kelany, Negem, Tohami & Heldal, "Granite-quarry survey in the Aswan region", NGU Special Publication 12 (QuarryScapes, 2009): Pharaonic quarrying largely exploited loose spheroidally weathered boulders; excavations at the Unfinished Obelisk quarry (Kelany 2003) yielded "massive amounts of charcoal, ash and burned mud bricks", read as heat being an important agent; ashy layers with charcoal are widespread; dolerite (and some granite) pounders occur in quantity with use-wear, and trenches along dolerite dykes are interpreted as pounder-production quarries ([academia.edu PDF](https://www.academia.edu/5407078/Granite_quarry_survey_in_the_Aswan_region_Egypt_shedding_new_light_on_ancient_quarrying)).
- *Inference.* The assessment's remark that a vindicated GEO-E001 is "compatible with fire-setting" now has a primary-source anchor. The same evidence cuts against the reading of ash deposits as casting-reagent signatures (the family of claims around GEO-C515/C516): at Aswan, ash co-occurs with quarry debris and pounder fragments, which is what fire-setting predicts. It also offers a mundane account of why so many dolerite spheres are present (GEO-C511, GEO-C537): they were made on site from dykes. I did not find the "narrow shafts" (GEO-C006) treated in this paper.

## 2. Best account

The Egyptian branch remains the only one tested with binder-aware petrography, and it failed for the alkali-aluminosilicate recipe (GEO-E018–E021). This pass adds two things on either side. Against casting: the one historical anchor the article leans on — the al-Maʾmūn salt — is unsourced in the proponent literature and absent from the two accessible medieval compilations, while the salt itself has a documented, testable natural origin (halite native to Giza nummulitic limestone; humidity-driven efflorescence; repeated cleaning). For casting, or at least for a mixed model: an independent paleomagnetic pilot found three of seven block locations sharing a N–S remanence direction — suggestive, methodologically fragile, never followed up. The honest state of GEO-C020 is unchanged (weakly supported), but the *shape* of what remains is clearer: the surviving proponent evidence is the C–S–H NMR result (GEO-E017), Barsoum's microconstituents with contested provenance (GEO-E012), and a paleomagnetic hint; the surviving conventional evidence is every direct petrographic look at a casing stone plus a geological explanation for the salt.

The Andean branch is the one where the two sides now genuinely engage. Independent pXRF work traces most Tiwanaku andesite to Ccapia and documents transport-stage carved blocks; proponents accept the Ccapia origin but reassign it to *sand* aggregate and disown the abandoned blocks. This is a cleaner disagreement than the ledger currently records, and it is decidable without touching the monuments: describe the Iwawe Stratum V deposit as its excavators did, and account for the piedras cansadas. The organic-matter SEM findings (GEO-C004) remain single-team and unreplicated.

Aswan stays where the assessment put it — a completeness problem for the pounding orthodoxy, not casting evidence — but with the orthodox complement (fire-setting on loose boulders, pounder manufacture on site) now anchored in a quarry survey rather than asserted.

Alternative and mixed explanations remain live: natural stone throughout with mortar/plaster finishing (the mainstream); natural blocks plus a minority of cast or re-agglomerated blocks (what Túnyi & El-hemaly literally conclude, and what Davidovits' "Imhotep adhesive" claim, GEO-C517, would also predict); and, for the Andes, natural andesite ashlars with a possible cast component in specific elements. None of these is excluded by the present record.

## 4. Narrative

- The article's sentence "Nobody has looked" (al-Maʾmūn) should be retired: two compilations have now been read and do not contain the salt; the salt itself has an independent 2026 characterisation and a geological explanation. Frame it as "the salt is real; its meaning is the dispute; the medieval attribution is so far unsupported."
- Add the paleomagnetic pilot as the one non-proponent physical hint toward a *mixed* pyramid — with its caveats stated — since it complicates the binary better than any existing plate caption.
- Replace the Andean paragraph's "no independent laboratory has ever re-examined them" with the actual state: independent provenance work exists, proponents accept the Ccapia origin and reinterpret it as sand — the fight is now over a lakeshore deposit and some abandoned blocks, which is a story readers can follow.
- Plates: IMG-GEO-P04 (Puma Punku H-blocks) caption should note the Ccapia provenance and the tired-stone counter-evidence. IMG-GEO-P01 (Unfinished Obelisk) caption should mention the charcoal/ash layers and fire-setting.
