# Working report — GEO-001 “Cast, Not Carved”

*Bounded pass, September 8, 2026. Focus: the surviving Bent Pyramid NMR result and the evidentiary chain for Pumapunku/Tiwanaku. This builds on, rather than repeats, the previous report.*

## 1. **Findings**

### F1. The Iwawi “stockpiled andesite sand” argument is an unsupported reinterpretation of a deposit previously identified as pumice, airfall ash, or stone-working debris.

**Source statements.** Davidovits and Davidovits quote the 2002 excavators as reporting that Iwawi Stratum V was virtually sterile and that microscopy of the available heavy-fraction sample “conclusively revealed volcanic pumice”; an airfall tephra origin was considered. They then quote Isbell’s 2013 interpretation: the layer was andesite sand, “probably from intensive work shaping imported stone blocks,” and was termed “sandy debitage.” The proponents reject these interpretations and infer that natural andesite sand from Ccapia was transported and stockpiled at Iwawi. But they report no new sample from Stratum V, no geochemical match between it and Ccapia, and conclude by saying that their proposed next step is to collect such samples and locate the exact source ([Davidovits & Davidovits 2020, pp. 38–43, Fig. 5 and Conclusion](https://www.geopolymer.org/wp-content/uploads/gpsa-2020-Davidovits-J-andesite-sand.pdf)). ([researchgate.net](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347188837_Ancient_geopolymers_in_South-American_Monuments_Part_IV_use_of_natural_andesite_volcanic_sand_not_crushed))

**Inference.** The Iwawi deposit does not presently supply the raw-material evidence claimed for the casting model. At most, it identifies a decisive sample that could be tested. As presently documented, the deposit fits two conventional alternatives—airfall tephra or debris from dressing imported andesite—at least as well as a casting-stockpile interpretation.

**Difference from the record.** This resolves the previous report’s leading question about Stratum V. It weakens the proposed proponent reply bearing on `GEO-C021` and `GEO-C030`; it also strengthens the importance of the transported andesite blocks already documented around Iwawi and Ccapia. The negative result should be recorded: **no compositional test tying Stratum V to Ccapia sand or to monument stone is presented in the proponent paper.**

### F2. `GEO-E006` is attached to the wrong lithology: its source analyzes Pumapunku red sandstone, not andesite.

**Source statement.** `SRC-DAVIDOVITS-2019-ML` studies sample PP4, taken directly from red sandstone slab No. 2 at Pumapunku. The paper compares PP4 with sandstone from Kausani, Amarillani, and Kallamarka and interprets its matrix as a ferro-sialate geopolymer. Andesite is not the analyzed monument material in this paper ([Davidovits, Huamán & Davidovits 2019, pp. 120–124; Materials and Methods, Fig. 1D](https://www.anticaciviltasarda.com/files/1556587723938-tiwanaku-geopolimeri.pdf)). ([anticaciviltasarda.com](https://www.anticaciviltasarda.com/files/1556587723938-tiwanaku-geopolimeri.pdf))

The separate Ceramics International paper, `SRC-DAVIDOVITS-2019-CI`, is the source that reports organic material in an andesite sample ([Davidovits, Huamán & Davidovits 2019, “Andesite samples”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272884219300288)). ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272884219300288?utm_source=openai))

**Inference.** The ledger currently makes the andesite case look independently anchored by two papers when only one of those papers analyzes andesite. The sandstone paper may still bear on the broader `GEO-C021`, but it cannot support `GEO-C004` or an evidence record titled “anomalies in Pumapunku andesite.”

**Difference from the record.** Corrects `GEO-E006`, which should be retitled and unlinked from `GEO-C004`. It also means the present andesite observation has one publication and one sample series, not two distinct analytical anchors.

### F3. The published andesite sample was loose surface debris selected by appearance, not a sample taken from an identified H-block.

**Source statement.** The Ceramics International paper says numerous andesite fragments lay outside the protected monument area and that PP1 was selected from this debris because it possessed a characteristically flat surface. The authors’ illustrated account likewise identifies PP1 as coming from the fresh broken portion of a loose fragment on the ground ([Ceramics International 45, “Andesite samples”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272884219300288); [Geopolymer Institute extended report, Fig. 21 and pp. 14–18](https://www.geopolymer.org/wp-content/uploads/TIWANAKU-PUMAPUNKU.pdf)). ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272884219300288?utm_source=openai))

The authors did cut an interior subsample, PP1C, and report similar carbon- and nitrogen-bearing material beneath mineral grains, which is stronger than a purely surface-confined observation. They nevertheless state that nitrogen was only semiquantitative and that they did not know whether the detected material was unreacted guano or a proposed binder ([extended report, pp. 14–17, Figs. 26–30](https://www.geopolymer.org/wp-content/uploads/TIWANAKU-PUMAPUNKU.pdf)). ([geopolymer.org](https://www.geopolymer.org/wp-content/uploads/TIWANAKU-PUMAPUNKU.pdf))

**Inference.** The internal location makes simple modern surface dirt an incomplete explanation, but the archaeological attribution remains insecure. The chain from PP1 to an H-block rests on visual selection of loose debris, without a monument object number, excavation context, or demonstrated physical refit. The laboratory result is therefore evidence about **a loose andesite-like fragment found at the site**, not yet secure evidence about the H-blocks shown in `IMG-GEO-P04`.

**Difference from the record.** Materially qualifies `GEO-E007`, `GEO-C004`, and `GEO-C021`. The present descriptions—especially the plate caption and article language—too readily transfer the PP1 findings to the H-block corpus as a whole.

### F4. The Bent Pyramid NMR result is substantially more self-qualified than `GEO-E017` and the adopted assessment indicate.

**Source statements.**

- Both principal ^29Si resonances found in the Bent Pyramid sample also occur in the Tura quarry sample. The authors’ artificial-stone inference depends partly on their relative intensities.
- The approximately −84 ppm shoulder is described as characteristic of C–S–H gel, but the authors explicitly say it does **not unambiguously prove** C–S–H because the expected Q1 resonance is absent, for which they have no explanation.
- The ^43Ca spectrum is primarily consistent with calcite, with only “possible slight broadening” attributed to a small amount of C–S–H.
- The authors concede that broader sampling of the extensive Tura and Giza quarry formations might produce spectra more similar to the pyramid sample.

These qualifications appear in the Results and Conclusions of the full paper ([MacKenzie et al. 2011, pp. 351–352, Figs. 1 and 3](https://www.anticaciviltasarda.com/files/10.1.1.870.591-piramidi-geopolimeri.pdf)). ([anticaciviltasarda.com](https://www.anticaciviltasarda.com/files/10.1.1.870.591-piramidi-geopolimeri.pdf))

**Inference.** The paper establishes that the single casing sample differs from the two quarry specimens in some spectral proportions. It does not uniquely identify a human-made C–S–H binder. Natural quarry variability is not merely an external criticism; it is an alternative the authors themselves leave open. The absence of a published rebuttal should not be treated as compensating for the under-sampled geological baseline.

**Difference from the record.** `GEO-E017` currently paraphrases the conclusion without its principal caveats and assigns moderate strength. A more accurate reading is **weak support for artificial binding, moderate evidence that this sample’s NMR spectrum differs from the two selected comparators**. This narrows the principal surviving positive argument for `GEO-C020`.

### F5. Pumapunku’s reconstructed architectural sequence weighs against casting the andesite components in their final positions, though not against off-site casting.

**Source statement.** Vranich’s reconstruction describes the andesite superstructure as roughly 150 separate finished blocks. Hoisting grips were cut through blocks so they could be lowered into position without damaging their edges. Decorative friezes were completed on each ashlar before assembly; matching decoration and joints allowed probable neighboring blocks to be refitted ([Vranich 2018, “Principles of Tiwanaku masonry and architecture,” especially Figs. 7–8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-018-0231-0)). ([nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-018-0231-0))

**Inference.** This is positive evidence for manufacture as discrete modules followed by handling and assembly. It contradicts a narrow model in which the architectural elements were poured directly into their final wall positions. It does **not** distinguish carving from blocks cast elsewhere and subsequently transported, so it should not be inflated into a general refutation of `GEO-C021`.

**Difference from the record.** Adds an unrepresented conventional evidence class to `GEO-C021` and the site-specific portion of `GEO-C022`. It also shows why the case should distinguish **cast in final position**, **precast nearby**, and **quarried and dressed** rather than using “casting” as one undifferentiated model.

## 2. **Best account**

The strongest current account is that the investigated monuments are predominantly constructed from natural stone that was quarried, transported, dressed, and assembled, while a minority-cast or composite model remains testable but has not yet been demonstrated with provenance-secure samples.

For Egypt, direct petrographic studies remain predominantly negative for casting. The Bent Pyramid NMR paper is the most important surviving exception, but its diagnostic force is lower than the edition suggests: its candidate C–S–H signal is incomplete, its calcium result is equivocal, and its natural baseline comprises only two quarry specimens. This makes `GEO-C020` weakly supported at most. Its appropriate crux is no longer “has anyone rebutted the NMR paper?” but “does the same candidate signal survive a broad, blinded geological baseline and an aged C–S–H positive control?”

For Pumapunku, the two peer-reviewed proponent papers must be separated. The sandstone paper used a directly located monument sample and therefore has the stronger archaeological provenance, although it admits that its proposed authigenic albite cannot currently be distinguished from naturally formed albite and relies on a sparse source comparison. The andesite paper reports a potentially interesting internal organic-bearing feature, but from a loose fragment selected outside the protected area. Its transfer to H-blocks is an attribution, not an observation. The Iwawi layer does not repair that problem: the available descriptions favor either volcanic ash or stone-working debris, while the stockpiled-Ccapia-sand interpretation has never been compositionally tested.

A mixed explanation remains possible: natural blocks with mortar, repair, coatings, inclusions, or a small number of manufactured components. But the evidence presently offers no securely identified monumental andesite object for which an engineered binder has been independently demonstrated. Thus `GEO-C022` can remain **unresolved** because the decisive tests have not been performed, but that verdict should not imply equal present support. The balance of observed evidence favors natural stone; the casting hypothesis survives principally as a falsifiable possibility awaiting properly controlled samples.

## 4. **Narrative**

The article should stop presenting “the Pumapunku analyses” as if both papers independently examined the H-blocks. A more accurate and more interesting account is:

> One paper sampled a known red sandstone slab. The other examined a loose andesite fragment selected from debris outside the protected area. Its internal organic-bearing features are intriguing, but nobody has yet shown that the fragment came from an H-block.

That single distinction would materially improve the article’s evidential honesty.

The Iwawi dispute is also narratively valuable because it turns an abstract methodology argument into one physical layer with three competing explanations: volcanic ash, stone-working debris, or imported casting feedstock. The proponent account itself concedes that the necessary samples have not been collected. This is a clearer crux for the Andean branch than generic calls for “independent re-analysis.”

For Egypt, the article should no longer describe the C–S–H NMR result simply as an unrebutted survivor. It should explain the anomalous shoulder, the absent expected resonance, and the authors’ concession about quarry variability. That makes the scientific disagreement more precise without dismissing the result.

**Existing plates and real objects:**

- **`IMG-GEO-P04`:** Retain, but revise the caption to say that the published andesite sample was loose debris and was not taken from one of the pictured H-blocks.
- **`IMG-GEO-P03`:** Useful beside a rewritten NMR paragraph, provided the caption makes clear that MacKenzie studied a Bent Pyramid casing sample, not the British Museum Khufu object pictured.
- **Pumapunku sandstone slab No. 2 / PP4 sampling point:** Davidovits et al. Fig. 1D would illustrate the stronger, directly located sample.
- **PP1 loose andesite fragment:** Fig. 21 in the proponents’ illustrated report would make the custody limitation immediately visible.
- **Iwawi “tired stone”:** A documented abandoned andesite block would show the strongest conventional counterevidence and the proponent attempt to assign those blocks to a later phase.
- **Vranich’s Model Stone 1 and modular refits:** These would illustrate that the andesite architecture was designed as a system of individually finished pieces, not simply a field of enigmatic isolated shapes.

## 5. **Next questions**

1. **What is PP1 demonstrably from?** Obtain collection permits, field photographs, coordinates, export documentation, remaining fragments, thin-section numbers, and any attempted physical or petrographic refit to a catalogued monument block.

2. **Can an identified H-block or documented museum fragment be tested?** A small detached fragment with an object number and conservation history would be substantially more diagnostic than another loose surface find.

3. **What exactly is Iwawi Stratum V?** Locate surviving samples and field records. Compare whole fractions—not only flotation heavy fractions—with Ccapia material, regional tephra, and experimentally generated dressing debris.

4. **Does the Bent Pyramid NMR signal exceed natural variability?** Repeat the analysis across a stratified suite of Tura and Dahshur limestone, with blind labels, replicate instruments, aged synthetic C–S–H controls, and prespecified criteria for Q1/Q2 resonances.

5. **Can the Pumapunku sandstone observations be reproduced?** Reanalyze PP4 or a provenance-secure equivalent alongside a substantially larger geological suite. Explicitly test the paper’s admitted natural alternative for authigenic albite and the claimed “fluidal” matrix.

6. **What does the modular construction sequence predict?** Compare mold seams, internal fabric orientation, aggregate sorting, lift/hoisting marks, unfinished surfaces, and decoration crossing block boundaries under three separate models: final-position casting, precasting, and quarry carving.

7. **Inspect the Protzen–Nair experiments directly.** Record stone lithology, tools, duration, achieved tolerances, which exterior and interior angles were replicated, and where the authors say the method remains unresolved.

8. **Locate the promised radiocarbon follow-up.** The andesite authors proposed gathering enough organic material for ^14C dating. This bounded pass did not locate a published result; that is a retrieval outcome, not proof that none exists.

Material worth preserving in the deeper record even if the article stays short includes the exact PP1 context, MacKenzie’s missing Q1 resonance, the three-way interpretation of Iwawi Stratum V, and Vranich’s evidence for pre-assembly finishing.
