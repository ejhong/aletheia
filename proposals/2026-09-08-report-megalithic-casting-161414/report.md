## 1. Findings

### F1. Engelbach 1922, read directly, does not match GEO-C502 — and it already contains a conventional reading of the "narrow shafts" (GEO-C006)

**Source statements** (Engelbach, *The Aswân Obelisk*, 1922, Project Gutenberg transcription — [§§4, 6, 13–15](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/59320/59320-h/59320-h.htm)):

- §13: Engelbach's own trial was one hour of hand pounding at the trench bottom; he removed "about five millimetres" over an area of one obelisk-foot × half the trench width. That is a depth-over-area estimate, i.e. an implicit volume, not a mass.
- §13: he states he is *certain* the pounders were "not used entirely by hand" for the trench, because hand work would be too slow and would not give the regular surface seen; his proposal is pounders lashed to rammers (the *mindâlah*), with men working from the trench top.
- §15: his 7.22-month trench estimate uses an *assumed* 8 mm/hour with rammers — a figure he did not measure — and one man per "double-foot" of trench, derived from the spacing of grooves on the trench floor (§14). I found no "count divided by four" heuristic anywhere in the monograph; the numbers-of-men calculations (§§22–24, 30) concern levering and hauling, not quarrying.
- §4: he documents fire-setting for the removal of the top rock layers: burnt and semi-burnt mud bricks, chips with the "pinkish-brown colour and crumbling texture peculiar to burnt granite," and reed stacks banked with brick.
- §6: the squarish holes at intervals along the trench, and the two deep holes C and D on Plate I, he reads as *test-shafts* "to study the quality of the granite" and to set out the obelisk perimeter, made while the top layers were still being removed.
- §§13–14: pounders are natural water-rounded dolerite balls, 8–13 in, 9–15 lb; the "bruising part" wears flat and a new part is then used; many were broken by the force of blows; he buried hundreds to stop souvenir-hunting.

**Inference.** GEO-C502 is half right (one self-conducted hour; no mass measurement) and half wrong (the workforce logic is trench-geometry, not a pounder count; and the monograph does not rest the orthodoxy on hand pounding at all — Engelbach rejects pure hand pounding and adds fire-setting). The "orthodoxy" GEO-C502 attacks is closer to Röder 1965 (see F2) than to Engelbach. GEO-C006 and GEO-C536 both describe the shafts as if no conventional function had ever been offered; Engelbach offered one in 1922, and it is the one piece of the record that speaks to their placement at trench bottoms (they predate the trench). The pounder-wear description (flattened bruising facets, percussion fracture) is the natural-history observation that GEO-C537's prediction ("mortar-and-pestle abrasion on glassy material") must be tested against.

**Differs from the record:** SRC-ENGELBACH-1922 is indexed but carries only GEO-E002 ("context"). The monograph directly *qualifies* GEO-C502, *undermines* the framing of GEO-C006/GEO-C536 (a prior conventional reading exists), and is *context* for GEO-C511/GEO-C537.

### F2. Kelany et al. 2009 (QuarryScapes, NGU SP 12) is now reached: fire-setting is documented at the Unfinished Obelisk quarry and across the Aswan granite quarries; pounding-only was Röder's conclusion

**Source statements** ([Kelany, Negem, Tohami & Heldal 2009, NGU Special Publication 12, pp. 87–98](https://static.ngu.no/upload/Publikasjoner/Special%20publication/SP12_s87-98.pdf)):

- p. 90: Pharaonic quarrying "largely involved the extraction of loose boulders," worked with dolerite pounders from dykes in the granitoids; the massive numbers of pounders in the Unfinished Obelisk quarry led *Röder (1965)* to conclude channelling was by pounding only; but recent excavations (Kelany 2003) found "massive amounts of charcoal, ash and burned mud bricks," so heat "must have been an important agent."
- p. 92: ashy layers with charcoal in quarry debris suggest fire-setting was widely applied in the Pharaonic period *outside* the Unfinished Obelisk quarry too; dolerite tools scattered across these areas.
- p. 93: numerous coarse-grained *granite* pounders in the Southern quarries whose surfaces "clearly show marks and wear from their use as pounders."
- p. 96: dolerite tool quarries described by Klemm & Klemm were hard to relocate; a possible new tool quarry near Philae with stone heaps of probable tool blanks (Fig. 14).
- p. 90 (S-3): trenches in dolerite/aplite dykes with roughly shaped pieces of pounder size, read as tool-production quarries.

**Inference.** The conventional account of Aswan is not "pounding only"; since 1922 (Engelbach §4) and 2003 (Kelany) it is boulder extraction + fire-setting + pounding + (later) iron. GEO-C001's low pounding rate stresses a pounding-only model that the orthodox literature itself has abandoned. GEO-C511 ("dolerite spheres served a purpose other than pounding granite") must now contend with (i) local pounder-blank quarries in dykes, (ii) reported use-wear, and (iii) pounders scattered wherever extraction debris occurs. Kelany describes use-wear qualitatively; no microwear study is cited — so GEO-C537's prediction remains untested rather than falsified.

**Differs from the record:** reopens the blocked `declined` entry for Kelany 2009 (now retrieved from the publisher). Adds an unrepresented conventional evidence class to GEO-C001, GEO-C002, GEO-C511, GEO-C537.

### F3. Túnyi & El-hemaly 2012 is now reached: a 7-location pilot, three sharing a N–S direction, one quarry location with no preferred direction — and the recipe it assumes is Nile mud + natron

**Source statements** ([Túnyi & El-hemaly, *Europhysics News* 43/6 (2012) 28–31, doi:10.1051/epn/2012604](https://www.europhysicsnews.org/articles/epn/pdf/2012/06/epn2012436p28.pdf)):

- p. 29: the casting model tested is that blocks "might have been produced from the mud of the Nile in situ" in wooden containers with natron, following Demortier [1]. Premise: in-situ set blocks → parallel N–S moments; transported blocks → random.
- pp. 29–30: samples from 6 pyramid locations (3 Khufu, 3 Khafre) plus quarry location 4 beside Khafre; "at least two samples" per position; thermal demagnetisation in Bratislava.
- p. 30: locations 1, 2, 5 share nearly identical paleodirections corresponding to the field in Egypt (Fig. 4, with 95% circle); locations 3 and 4 (quarry) show a susceptibility jump at ~400 °C and "impossible to identify any preferred direction"; location 7 resembles 3/4; location 6's direction is "irrelevant" owing to low-temperature phase change.
- p. 31 (Conclusion): three locations "may have been produced in situ"; one Khafre block is natural and from the adjacent quarry; even if the concrete technique was used, the pyramids were "a mixture of natural and artificial" blocks; "a more thorough investigation" is needed.

**Inference (mine, separately).** (i) The recipe assumed — Nile mud — is a *fourth* casting chemistry in this case (after alkali-aluminosilicate, C–S–H, and natron/waterglass) and is already excluded by every petrographic study in the ledger (GEO-E018–E021), which find fossiliferous marine limestone, not clay. The paleomagnetic test is recipe-agnostic only if a cold-set slurry acquires a stable, field-aligned remanence at all — the paper asserts the depositional/thermal mechanism but does not demonstrate acquisition in a chemically setting limestone paste. (ii) The one in-situ natural sample (location 4) yielded *no* preferred direction; the paper treats this as characterising natural rock, but it equally shows that the natural baseline was not established — one quarry site, no stable direction, no D/I numbers, no α95 values in text, no statement of how block orientation was recorded. (iii) A conventional alternative not considered: blocks laid on their bedding planes preserve inclination regardless of rotation, and declination clustering could arise if masons habitually kept quarry-face orientation during sledging. (iv) This is a pilot by non-proponents in a physics-society magazine, not a refereed paleomagnetic journal. It is exactly the motivation for GEO-R011 and should be entered as *weak* support, with its caveats, not as a result.

**Differs from the record:** reopens the blocked `declined` entry (EPN PDF now retrieved). Bears on GEO-C020 (weak support), GEO-C589 (context: demonstrates the method is applicable), GEO-R011 (design: the pilot's gaps are the baseline and orientation control the research item already specifies).

### F4. Hemeda & Sonbol 2020 is now reached: halite is a minor *bulk* constituent of Khufu backing limestone by XRD and thin section, in a rock described as fossiliferous biomicrite

**Source statements** ([Hemeda & Sonbol, *Heritage Science* 8:8 (2020), "The backing limestone blocks (Cheops great pyramid)" and thin-section sections; Table 3, Figs. 16, 21](https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-020-0356-9)): the Khufu backing limestone is calcite with minor iron oxides and quartz and "rare" dolomite, opaques and halite (XRD, Table 3); in thin section it is a very fine-grained biomicrite with abundant microfossils infilled by recrystallised calcite/dolomite, nummulites to 5 mm; "during storage over a longer period, various salts effloresce at the surface." The Khafre and Menkaure joining mortars are gypsum/lime based and also contain halite. The paper cites Klemm & Klemm 2010 for quarry geography only; it analyses *block* samples and no unworked bedrock.

**Inference.** This moves the halite question from surface (GEO-E030, GEO-E031) to bulk: NaCl is inside the block fabric, detectable by XRD. That is what GEO-C590's logic would call a positive bulk-chlorine signal — but for a marine limestone that Klemm & Klemm (second-hand, GEO-E032) say contains primary halite, bulk halite is predicted by both accounts and remains non-diagnostic. The petrographic description (microfossils with recrystallised infill, sedimentary matrix) independently corroborates the natural-fabric observations of GEO-E018/E019/E021 for backing stone. The missing comparator is still bedrock/quarry face (GEO-R010).

**Differs from the record:** reopens the blocked `declined` entry. Bears on GEO-C506 (undermines the "comparable natural limestone lacks it" half by showing halite native to the block fabric), GEO-C906 (supports, still short of bedrock), GEO-C590 (qualifies: bulk chlorine is not a discriminator for salt-bearing marine limestones), GEO-C020 (undermines, weak).

### F5. Legon 1993 is now reached: salt incrustations were removed from the Queen's Chamber walls before 1993

**Source statement** ([Legon, "The Air-Shafts in the Great Pyramid," *Discussions in Egyptology* 27 (1993), p. 36 and n. 5](https://gizamedia.rc.fas.harvard.edu/documents/legon_de_27_1993.pdf)): the lower part of the King's Chamber southern shaft has a rough appearance "certainly caused by an incrustation of salts, similar to those recently removed from the walls of the Queen's Chamber itself," citing Z. Hawass, appendix to the 1990 reprint of Petrie's *Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh*, p. 103.

**Inference.** A second, earlier, dated cleaning episode (before 1993, on Hawass's authority) joins the undocumented 1995–97 intervention in GEO-E034. Every taste-test or visual salt report inside the Great Pyramid since c. 1990 is therefore a reading taken after at least one removal. It also records salt in a shaft lined with "fine white limestone" — casing-grade Tura-type stone — which is consistent with halite mobility in this lithology generally.

**Differs from the record:** reopens the blocked `declined` entry. Supports GEO-C594; qualifies GEO-C506 and GEO-E026 (taste tests 1984–2003 straddle a cleaning).

### F6. Radiocarbon dating of pyramid mortar exists (Bonani et al. 2001) — bears on GEO-C576

**Source statements.** The Cambridge record for [Bonani, Haas, Hawass, Lehner et al., *Radiocarbon* 43(3) (2001) 1297–1320](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/radiocarbon/article/radiocarbon-dates-of-old-and-middle-kingdom-monuments-in-egypt/A967302ADD527BFEB9226457682C0B4A) states (abstract only shown): over 450 organic samples were collected 1984–1995 from Old and Middle Kingdom monuments "from secure context" and the resulting chronology compared with the historical one. The project's own summary ([AERA, "How Old Are the Pyramids?"](https://aeraweb.org/projects/how-old-are-the-pyramids/)) says the 1984 dates averaged 374 years older than the historical midpoints, the 1995 dates 100–200 years older with a ~400-year scatter for Khufu, attributes this to an old-wood effect in charcoal used for mortar preparation, and concludes the Great Pyramid falls in the historical era. I did not open the full paper; the sampling contexts (mortar between blocks vs. surface finds) are stated only in the secondary summary.

**Inference.** GEO-C576 asserts "wood fragments found on the ground rather than in masonry." The Bonani programme sampled organics from mortar contexts on the project's account; if the full paper confirms in-masonry sampling, GEO-C576 is factually wrong on that point. Interestingly, the same data are ambivalent for the natron/wood-ash line (GEO-C516): the authors themselves speculate about Old Kingdom wood depletion driven by mortar, copper and bread — a fuel-demand argument that does not need casting.

**Differs from the record:** GEO-C576 has no verdict and one anchor; this is its first independent counter-source. Enter only after the full paper is opened.

## 2. Best account

The Aswan branch of this case now looks different. The ledger has treated the "pounding orthodoxy" as Engelbach's, and Engelbach's evidentiary base as a one-hour trial. Read directly, Engelbach rejected hand pounding for the trench, proposed rammers, documented fire-setting from burnt bricks and burnt chips, and interpreted the deep shafts as prospecting pits made before the trench existed. Kelany and colleagues' 2003 excavation added massive charcoal and ash at the Unfinished Obelisk quarry and across other Pharaonic granite workings, plus pounder-blank quarries in the dolerite dykes. So the measured 216 cm³/h (GEO-C001) undercuts a pounding-only model that Röder held and that Engelbach did not. The residual anomalies here — the untested scoop-mark test block, the shafts' geometry, pounder microwear — are real research gaps but small ones, and none points to casting rather than to fire, rammers and labour. The natron-processing reading of the shafts (GEO-C536) must now beat a specific prior interpretation rather than fill a vacuum.

On Egypt's stone, the new material is two-edged but net conventional. Halite is inside the blocks, not only on their surfaces (Hemeda & Sonbol), and the blocks' fabric is fossiliferous biomicrite. Both accounts predict bulk salt if the plateau strata carry it; only bedrock and Tura quarry-face sampling (GEO-R010) can break the tie, and Klemm & Klemm remains unread. Surface-salt observations are further compromised by a pre-1993 cleaning on top of the 1995–97 one. The paleomagnetic pilot is the one non-proponent result that leans toward casting, and it is genuinely interesting: three locations with a shared field-aligned direction. But it tests a Nile-mud recipe that the petrography already excludes, has no natural-limestone baseline (its single quarry site gave no stable direction), reports no numbers, and does not address bedding-plane laying or orientation control. It motivates GEO-R011; it does not pre-empt it.

The strongest remaining pro-casting position is unchanged from the adopted assessment: the low-percentage C–S–H binder of GEO-E017 has never been tested for with a matched Tura suite and an aged positive control, and the previous report showed even that result is heavily self-qualified. The strongest conventional position is stronger than before: at every point where the ledger recorded a "gap" at Aswan, the primary and survey literature had already filled it with fire and rammers; and the salt story has shifted from "anomaly" to "predicted by both, discriminable only at bedrock."

A mixed explanation remains open in principle — natural blocks with lime-gypsum mortars (themselves manufactured lime products, GEO-C520) and possibly some repaired or reconstituted elements — but no securely provenanced monument object yet carries an independently demonstrated engineered binder.
