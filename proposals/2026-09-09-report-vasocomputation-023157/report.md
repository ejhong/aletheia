# Working report — VASO-001 "State, Not Scar" (pass of 2026-09-09, successor to the inbox intake)

## 1. Findings

Bounded pass aimed at the two cheap "desk items" the edition ranks third and fourth (VASO-R008, VASO-R009), plus the primary literature behind VASO-E007 and any existing precedent for the keystone experiment (VASO-R001). Web access was exhausted mid-pass (see §6), so several items are abstract-level or snippet-level and are labelled as such.

**F1. The phentolamine "study" is located — and it is a review of the proponents' own lab data, not an independent primary paper (bears on VASO-R009, VASO-C002, VASO-C003, VASO-C020).**
- *Observed.* Banks, Jacobs, Gevirtz & Hubbard, "Effects of Autogenic Relaxation Training on Electromyographic Activity in Active Myofascial Trigger Points," *J Musculoskeletal Pain* 6(4), 1998, doi 10.1300/J094V06N04_03 ([academia.edu copy, OCR'd full text](https://www.academia.edu/8617037/Effects_of_Autogenic_Relaxation_Training_on_Electromyographic_Activity_in_Active_Myofascial_Trigger_Points)). Discussion section: the group states it had "previously established that the TrP activity was not blocked by curare … but was blocked by phentolamine," citing its reference 9 = Hubbard DR, "Chronic and recurrent muscle pain: pathophysiology and treatment, and review of pharmacologic studies," *J Musculoskeletal Pain* 4(1/2):123–143, 1996, doi 10.1300/J094v04n01_08. The tandfonline landing page for that DOI was not accessible to me; its abstract, seen only as a search-engine snippet, states that phentolamine and phenoxybenzamine eliminate needle-EMG spike activity in the TrP nidus and reduce pain report.
- *Observed.* Rivner, "The neurophysiology of myofascial pain syndrome," *Curr Pain Headache Rep* 5:432–440, 2001 ([abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11916-001-0054-6)) independently summarises the same result and adds that the spindle theory "may explain the effects of a-adrenergic antagonists" but not the EMG waveform, which resembles endplate spikes.
- *Observed.* A rabbit primary exists: Chen, Chen, Kuan, Chung & Hong, "Phentolamine effect on the spontaneous electrical activity of active loci in a myofascial trigger spot of rabbit skeletal muscle," *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 79:790–794, 1998 — cited in the Ge et al. 2011 review below and in several other secondary sources I saw; I did **not** open its abstract.
- *Inference.* VASO-R009's question ("does it exist?") is answered: yes, as (a) a 1996 review in a non-indexed Haworth journal reporting the Hubbard lab's own infusion data, and (b) a 1998 rabbit study from Hong's group. Neither is a vascular measurement. Alpha-blockade quieting the nidus is consistent with Hubbard's sympathetic-intrafusal hypothesis, with sympathetic facilitation of endplate ACh release (Ge 2011's reading), *and* with a vascular account (locally infused phentolamine is a vasodilator). Credible; low diagnosticity between the competing accounts. The article's line "no primary paper has been located" should change to "located; a self-reported pharmacologic series plus a rabbit study; not independently replicated in humans."

**F2. Relaxation training lowers nidus EMG — the "reverse" half the essay mentioned now has a record (bears on VASO-C003, VASO-E002).**
- *Observed.* Banks et al. 1998 (link above): 21 chronic-pain patients, upper trapezius, needle EMG at TrP and adjacent non-TrP fibres across baseline / autogenic relaxation / post-relaxation / mental-arithmetic stressor; Table 1 (OCR'd — digits should be checked against a clean copy) gives TrP means of roughly 13.4 → 7.6 → 8.7 → 12.9 µV; skin conductance fell during relaxation.
- *Inference.* Adds a bidirectional autonomic-modulation record from the same lineage as VASO-E001/E002 (stress up, relaxation down). Same caveats: one lab, unblinded, skeletal-muscle electrophysiology, no vascular readout.

**F3. "Confined to the nidus, absent in adjacent muscle" is overstated: endplate noise occurs at non-tender endplate-zone sites too (corrects VASO-C002).**
- *Observed.* Simons, Hong & Simons, "Endplate potentials are common to midfiber myofascial trigger points," *Am J Phys Med Rehabil* 81:212–222, 2002 ([PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11989519/)): 10 subjects, 11 muscles; endplate noise at 11/11 TrP sites, 4/11 endplate-zone sites outside TrPs, 0 taut-band sites outside the endplate zone; conclusion: endplate noise is "characteristic of, but is not restricted to" the trigger-point region.
- *Observed.* Ge, Fernández-de-las-Peñas & Yue, "Myofascial trigger points: spontaneous electrical activity and its consequences…," *Chin Med* 6:13, 2011 ([open access](https://cmjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1749-8546-6-13)) reads the SEA as excess endplate ACh release from extrafusal endplates rather than spindles, and cites Kuan et al. 2002 (rabbit) showing botulinum toxin reduces SEA.
- *Inference.* VASO-C002 should be reworded from "confined to a millimeter-scale nidus, absent in adjacent non-tender muscle" to "more prevalent at TrPs than at non-tender endplate-zone sites, and absent off the endplate zone." Hubbard–Berkoff's silent control sites may simply have been off the endplate zone. There is also an unresolved conflict in the record: Hubbard reports curare did not block the activity, while Kuan 2002 reports botulinum toxin (which blocks ACh release) did — both bear on whether the nidus is cholinergic at all.

**F4. The primary Doppler numbers behind VASO-E007 — and the 31% figure the ledger lacks (bears on VASO-C005, VASO-C020).**
- *Observed.* Ballyns, Shah, Gebreab, Gerber & Sikdar, "Objective Sonographic Measures for Characterizing Myofascial Trigger Points Associated With Cervical Pain," *J Ultrasound Med* 30(10):1331–1340, 2011 ([PMC author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3493620/)): n=44 with acute cervical pain; vibration sonoelastography TrP area 0.57 ± 0.20 cm² active vs 0.36 ± 0.16 latent vs 0.17 ± 0.22 normal; retrograde diastolic flow in 27/49 active sites (55%), 21 latent (40%), 15 normal (31%); pulsatility index median 8.3 active vs 3.0 normal; resistivity index no difference; vessels were "in the close vicinity" of the TrP on colour Doppler.
- *Inference.* This is the strongest vascular finding in the ledger's favour and it is graded, not categorical: nearly a third of palpably normal sites show the "abnormal" waveform. The vessel interrogated is a colour-Doppler-visible artery (hundreds of µm), and high PI signals high downstream resistance — consistent with distal arteriolar constriction (the hypothesis) *or* mechanical compression by a taut band (the conventional account). It is also the correct primary to cite where the article currently says the imaging review's characterisation is "second-hand."

**F5. Objective stiffness change after pressure alone, within one session, exists in the literature — the nearest published precedent to the keystone experiment (bears on VASO-C011, VASO-C004, VASO-R001, VASO-C001).**
- *Observed.* Olewnik et al., "Ultrasound elastography to guide compression therapy for gastrocnemius trigger points in runners," *Sci Rep* 15:41950, 25 Nov 2025, doi 10.1038/s41598-025-25902-8 ([open access](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-25902-8)): 44 runners screened, 30 with gastrocnemius MTrPs, 103 MTrPs (mean 4.7 per person by palpation vs 3.4 by ultrasound); ischemic compression in 30-s bouts with 10-s rests, up to three rounds; immediately after, 75/103 MTrPs undetectable by palpation and imaging, 21/103 non-palpable with reduced elastographic stiffness, 7/103 persistent; none of the 96 recurred at 24 h. Radiologist blinded to outcome but not to initial palpation; strain (not shear-wave) elastography; no control group; Doppler explicitly not performed; no breath manipulation.
- *Inference.* This supplies what VASO-C011 lacked — one objective, imaged record that a palpated stiff focus can disappear within ~2 minutes of pressure and stay gone for a day, which fits "state" rather than "remodelling" whichever state it is. It also *qualifies* VASO-C004/VASO-E014: the essay's claim that "pressure alone does not seem to release them" is contradicted here, since no breath instruction was given and 73% resolved. It does not touch the vascular attribution: there is no perfusion channel and no time-lock. It sharpens VASO-R001 to "add Doppler, time-resolution, a breath factor, sham sites and a no-treatment arm to a design that has already been shown feasible." Finally, the palpation-vs-ultrasound gap (4.7 vs 3.4) is a small datum for VASO-C030/VASO-C001.

**F6. Sympathetic vasoconstrictor hyper-reactivity at latent trigger points (bears on VASO-C005, VASO-C020).**
- *Observed (abstract via PubMed search snippet only; the PubMed page returned empty on fetch).* Zhang, Ge, Yue, Kimura & Arendt-Nielsen, "Attenuated skin blood flow response to nociceptive stimulation of latent myofascial trigger points," *Arch Phys Med Rehabil* 90:325–332, 2009: glutamate bolus into a latent MTrP vs a non-MTrP in brachioradialis; attenuated skin blood-flow response at MTrPs, interpreted as increased sympathetic vasoconstrictor activity. The Ge 2011 review (link above) summarises the same lineage as showing decreased muscle and skin blood supply after noxious MTrP stimulation.
- *Inference.* The first record in the ledger showing an *autonomic-vascular* abnormality measured at a trigger point rather than inferred. Skin flow, not intramuscular; provocation-evoked, not resting; latent points. Supports the "vascular-autonomic" half of VASO-C020 weakly; silent on the latch.

**F7. Densification timescale still not retrieved (VASO-R008 stays open).**
- *Observed.* "Painful Connections: Densification Versus Fibrosis of Fascia," *Curr Pain Headache Rep* 18:8, 2014, doi 10.1007/s11916-014-0441-4 ([abstract only, paywalled](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11916-014-0441-4)): densification is described as "an alteration that is easily reversible" via diet, exercise and overuse changes; fibrosis is not. No timescale in the abstract. The ClinicalTrials.gov record NCT06060925 (seen only as a search-snippet reference list) points to Cowman, Schmidt, Raghavan & Stecco, "Viscoelastic Properties of Hyaluronan in Physiological Conditions," *F1000Res* 4:622, 2015, as the physics paper; not opened.
- *Inference.* Nothing yet establishes that hyaluronan de-densification can occur in seconds; "easily reversible" in this literature refers to weeks of therapy or lifestyle. The `declined` Stecco entry can be partially reopened with the 2014 DOI as a retrieved (abstract-level) anchor, but the decisive question is unanswered.

**F8. Mazza 2021 abstract directly observed (bears on VASO-E007's provenance note).**
- *Observed (LWW abstract page content, via search result; full text not opened).* 1,762 abstracts screened, 69 full-text, 33 included, 2000–2021, QUADAS quality assessment; active and latent MTrPs "quantifiably distinct" from healthy tissue on biomechanical and blood-flow measures; calls for diagnostic-accuracy and reproducibility work; ultrasound preferred on cost and practicality.
- *Inference.* The ledger's note that this characterisation was second-hand can be softened to "abstract observed; full text still to be opened."

## 2. Best account

The pass does not move the case verdict, but it changes the shape of two rungs. On the observation rung, the "electrical nidus" is less sharply bounded than the edition says: endplate noise is more *prevalent* at trigger points, not *exclusive* to them, and the dominant published reading is excess endplate ACh release in skeletal muscle (F3). The autonomic handle on that nidus is now better documented from both directions — mental arithmetic raises it, autogenic relaxation lowers it, alpha-blockade quiets it (F1, F2) — but every one of those records comes from the Hubbard–Gevirtz lineage or Hong's rabbit model, and every one is a skeletal-muscle EMG readout. The mechanism they most directly support is "a stress-sensitive, sympathetically facilitated motor endplate," which is the conventional guarding account wearing autonomic clothes, not a vascular latch.

On the mechanism rung, the vascular evidence is real but graded and indirect. Feeding arteries near active points show high pulsatility and retrograde diastolic flow more often than at normal sites — yet 31% of normal sites show it too (F4); latent points show exaggerated sympathetic vasoconstriction to a noxious stimulus (F6). Both fit "something downstream is constricted" and both fit "a contracted taut band is compressing the bed." Nothing here separates arteriolar smooth muscle from skeletal-muscle compression.

The one genuinely new lever is F5. A blinded radiologist watched 73% of palpated stiff foci vanish on strain elastography within about two minutes of thumb pressure, with none returning at 24 hours. That is objective evidence for the *state* half of "state, not scar" — the first the ledger has — and it arrived without any breath component, which weakens the two-key framing of VASO-C004: pressure appears sufficient, at least for gastrocnemius points in runners. Whether the exhale adds anything, whether perfusion changes, and whether the change is time-locked to anything remain unmeasured. The mixed explanation now looks strongest: a centrally maintained, sympathetically modulated endplate/guarding state that pressure can switch off, sitting in tissue whose local circulation is measurably abnormal, with the causal direction between the two undetermined. The latch-in-arteriole attribution (VASO-C020) neither gains nor loses from this pass; the arteriole record from VASO-S001 still leans against it.

## 4. Narrative

- Replace "no primary paper has been located" (phentolamine) with the accurate, less flattering version: the human result lives in Hubbard's own 1996 pharmacology review, the rabbit result in Chen 1998, and neither measures blood flow.
- Temper "confined to a one-to-two-millimetre nidus, and none in the non-tender sites" with Simons 2002's 4-of-11 figure; the honest sentence is "more common at knots than at non-tender endplate sites, and absent away from endplates."
- Give the reader the Ballyns numbers directly — 55% / 40% / 31% retrograde flow, PI 8.3 vs 3.0 — so the vascular signature is seen as a gradient across ordinary muscle rather than a marker.
- Add a short paragraph on Olewnik 2025 as "the half of the keystone experiment that has been run": pressure alone, elastography, blinded reader, most points gone in minutes and still gone next day — no breath, no blood-flow channel, no controls. This is the strongest objective support the article has for "state, not scar," and its most direct challenge to "release comes with breath."
- Plates: IMG-VASO-P02 (smooth muscle) still fits the latch section. A useful new plate would be an elastography/Doppler pair over a trigger point. Olewnik 2025 is published under a 2025 "The Author(s)" copyright in an open-access journal — Fig. 3 (compression technique) or Fig. 5 (elimination outcomes) could be candidates *if* the licence is confirmed CC BY. Ballyns 2011 Fig. 2 (Doppler waveform grades) is an NIH author manuscript and its reuse terms should be checked before use. I inspected neither image file itself, only their captions.
