# Working report — AMZ-001 "The Emptied Amazon" (bounded pass)

## 1. Findings

**F1. The manuscript situation behind AMZ-C002 is more specific than the record's "Medina/Muñoz branch" label — and the record's primary Carvajal source can now be keyed to an open edition.**
I opened the full text of José Toribio Medina's 1894 edition, *Descubrimiento del Río de las Amazonas* (Seville: Rasco), on the Internet Archive: [archive.org/details/descubrimientod00carvgoog](https://archive.org/details/descubrimientod00carvgoog) (full-text stream at `descubrimientod00carvgoog_djvu.txt`; the fetch was truncated at ~70k characters, so I read the Introducción, pp. vii–xv, not the Relación itself).
*Source states (Introducción, pp. ix–xi and notes 1–4):* two copies of the Relación are known — one inserted by Gonzalo Fernández de Oviedo in his *Historia general y natural de las Indias*, "haciéndole algunos arreglos" (Medina's words; note 1 locates it at t. IV, pp. 541–574 of the 1851 edition), and the manuscript owned by the Duke of T'Serclaes de Tilly, printed by Medina for the first time; an *incomplete* copy also exists in the Muñoz collection at the Real Academia de la Historia (p. x). Medina judges the T'Serclaes manuscript not autograph but in a period hand (note 4), collated both witnesses, and concludes both are by the same author, the variants arising either from copies taken by dictation through inexpert amanuenses or from the author himself adding or suppressing words and phrases (p. xi). He also notes Antonio de Herrera followed the Relación almost verbatim (p. x, note 2), and quotes Carvajal's own scope statement (Relación p. 5, at note 5) that everything before Orellana's arrival at Pizarro's camp is hearsay and everything after is eyewitness.
*My inference:* (a) AMZ-C002 should name three witnesses — Oviedo (as edited by Oviedo), T'Serclaes/Medina (Medina's base text), and Muñoz (incomplete) — rather than a two-branch "Medina/Muñoz" family; (b) Medina's own editorial judgement is that the variants are transmission/author-revision effects within one authorship, which is consistent with the case's "numbers grew in the telling" reading but does not by itself tell us *which* witness carries the original figures; (c) Herrera is *not* an independent witness and should not be counted toward AMZ-C003-style corroboration. This bears directly on AMZ-R002 and gives SRC-CARVAJAL-1542 (currently `unverified`, no keys) a concrete identifier.

**F2. The primary Xingu study is not in the index; the 1770 terminus in AMZ-C011 is not in it either.**
I opened Heckenberger et al. 2008, "Pre-Columbian Urbanism, Anthropogenic Landscapes, and the Future of the Amazon," *Science* 321:1214–1217 (PDF mirror: [acervo.socioambiental.org/…/0pd00267.pdf](https://acervo.socioambiental.org/sites/default/files/documents/0pd00267.pdf)).
*Source states:* 28 prehistoric residential sites identified, most in two "galactic clusters" studied for ~750–450 yr B.P.; "After ~500 to 400 yr B.P., early European colonialism resulted in substantial depopulation and forest expansion" (p. 1214–1215); walled towns with ditches 1–3 m deep and 5–10 m wide plus palisades, central plazas 120–150 m across, roads 20–50 m wide within settlements and 10–20 m between them; a regional peer polity of ≥15 clusters over ≥20,000 km² with an estimated population of ≥50,000 (>2.5 persons/km²) (pp. 1215–1216, ref. 25); note 11 says patterns "continue beyond" AD 1500 in many areas.
*My inference:* this is the primary anchor AMZ-E019 currently lacks (it rests on SRC-SCIENCE-XINGU-2024, a news feature). But the 2008 paper gives no "1770" date; that figure must come from later Heckenberger work or from the feature. AMZ-C011's Xingu clause therefore needs a source that actually states it, or should be softened to "occupied at contact, with continuity of layout."

**F3. The Aquiry/Acre region was not abandoned after the earthwork peak — a contact-era horizon exists there, which changes how AMZ-E003 should be read.**
I opened Iriarte et al. 2020, "Geometry by Design," *Journal of Computer Applications in Archaeology* 3(1), [doi:10.5334/jcaa.45](https://journal.caa-international.org/articles/10.5334/jcaa.45).
*Source states (Abstract; Introduction; Discussion):* Mound Villages (~AD 1000–1650) in SE Acre succeeded the Geoglyphs (400 BC–AD 950); excavated circular/elliptical villages date ~AD 1000–1650 with most dates AD 1200–1500; rectangular types have few dates, pointing "from ~AD 1400 to colonial times"; and after geoglyph construction ceased (~AD 950) "this region of SW Amazonia was not abandoned." It also cites Carvajal's wide roads leading inland as the earliest historical attestation of Amazonian road networks.
*My inference:* AMZ-E003 ("a millennium before Orellana") is correct about the *geoglyph* florescence but should be paired with the fact that the same region carries a later, contact-adjacent settlement tradition. Whether the 432 earthworks in SRC-PARSSINEN-2026 include Mound Villages, and how many, is a question I did not resolve (I did not reopen that paper this pass).

**F5. A published pollen synthesis directly competes with AMZ-C015.**
Bush et al. 2021, "Widespread reforestation before European influence on Amazonia," *Science* 372:484–487 — abstract seen only via search snippets from [science.org](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf3870) and PubMed; full text not reached. *Snippet states:* on 39 Amazonian fossil pollen records, no synchronous reforestation event follows European arrival; at most sites land abandonment and regrowth began ~300–600 years before European arrival. The University of Reading release (opened: [phys.org, 24 May 2021](https://phys.org/news/2021-05-pre-columbus-climate-amazon-population-decline.html)) adds co-author Mayle's suggestion that climate change may have driven decline of some societies centuries before Europeans, and that the team found no evidence the "Orbis spike" was caused by Amazonian reforestation.
*My inference:* the radiocarbon-SPD proxy (SRC-ARROYOKALIN-2021: logistic growth to carrying capacity, terminal decline attributed to sampling bias plus post-contact collapse) and the pollen proxy (Bush 2021: abandonment beginning 1000–1200 CE at most sites) disagree about the pre-contact trajectory. The record currently presents only one side. This is the most consequential unrecorded tension I found; it needs the full paper.

## 2. Best account

The record's core thesis — a heterogeneous, corridor-and-hotspot Amazon — survives this pass, but the *temporal* story needs a second axis. The primary sources I opened point to two distinct kinds of disappearance. Several of the most impressive systems ended well before 1492: Upano (by ~600 CE, with a later, different reoccupation), the Acre geoglyph tradition (~AD 950), Casarabe (~AD 1400), and — if Bush et al. 2021 holds — a broad pollen signal of abandonment beginning three to six centuries before contact. Others ran to the contact horizon and beyond: the Acre Mound Villages (to ~1650 and "colonial times"), the Upper Xingu clusters (depopulation only after ~500–400 BP), and, per the existing record, Belterra, Marajó's Cacoal, and the middle Solimões. Carvajal's 1542 corridor sits in the second group; the basin as a whole belongs to both.

That matters for the founding question. "Describing a world that died between the visits" is well supported for the reaches Carvajal actually saw — independent testimony (AMZ-E007), contact-era occupation at Santarém/Marajó/Xingu (AMZ-C011), and Newson's regional collapse figures (AMZ-E008) carry it. But the basin-wide extrapolation now has a documented competitor: some decline was endogenous or climatic and preceded Europeans, and two aggregate proxies (radiocarbon SPD vs. pollen) currently disagree about how much. A mixed account — pre-contact reorganization in some regions, catastrophic post-contact collapse in the riverine corridors — fits more of the evidence than either "everything died after 1542" or "it was already emptying."

On the text: Medina's own collation supports the case's posture (one author; variants from copying and revision), which weakens "invention" as an explanation of the qualitative picture without settling which witness preserves the original numbers. Oviedo's arrangements and Medina's admission that the T'Serclaes copy is not autograph mean neither branch is privileged a priori; that is exactly what AMZ-R002 is for.

The skeptical position remains strong where it always was — proportions and representativeness — and gains a new lever from F4/F5: the archaeology that best answers the carrying-capacity objection is not contact-era archaeology.
