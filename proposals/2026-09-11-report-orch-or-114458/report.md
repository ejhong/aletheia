# Working report — ORCH-001 *Collapse, Not Computation*

Bounded pass. I went after four directions that looked underworked in `index`: (1) whether the xenon nuclear-spin anomaly has a *quantum but non-Orch-OR* explanation in the primary literature; (2) whether ORCH-C031's collapse bound rests on the radiation channel alone; (3) what the 2026 theory/experiment literature on tryptophan networks actually measures; (4) the newest proponent statement and how it handles the contradictory animal study. ## 1. Findings

**F1. There is a published, quantitative, *quantum* explanation of the xenon isotope result that requires neither microtubules nor objective reduction — and it carries a cheap decisive test.** Smith, Zadeh-Haghighi, Salahub & Simon model a naturally occurring radical pair, specifically an [O₂·⁻ TrpH·⁺] pair whose singlet–triplet interconversion is modulated by hyperfine coupling to the xenon nucleus; they reproduce the Li 2018 per-isotope potencies, quoting the published LRR-ED₅₀ values as 70(4)%, 72(5)%, 99(5)% and 105(7)% for ¹³²Xe, ¹³⁴Xe, ¹³¹Xe and ¹²⁹Xe ([Sci Rep 11:6287, Results, "Quantifying experimental anesthetic potency"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-85673-w)). Critically, the model is field-dependent: agreement with the measured potencies holds only for B ∈ [0, 2525] µT, so at fields ~two orders above geomagnetic the isotope ordering should change, with ¹³¹Xe becoming *more* potent than observed ([same paper, Discussion and Fig. 8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-85673-w)).
*How this differs from the record:* ORCH-C025 is anchored seven ways and framed in the edition as "hard to fit into a pharmacology with no quantum degrees of freedom in it," with the proponent reading (ORCH-E034) on one side and the S002 audit on the other. The ledger's ORCH-C004 is written as the fully-classical alternative. This paper occupies a third position the claim set does not currently hold: spin-chemistry quantum biology with no collapse, no coherent tubulin lattice, and no consciousness commitment. It also independently reproduces the four per-isotope numbers that ORCH-E040 flags as calculated rather than measured — a usable cross-check on S002's extraction.
*My inference (labelled):* the xenon line's diagnosticity for ORCH-C002 should fall further. It discriminates "quantum degrees of freedom matter somewhere in anesthetic action" from "no quantum degrees of freedom," which is a real and interesting discrimination — but it does nothing to localize the effect to microtubules, and there is now a competitor with an explicit mechanism and a falsifiable field prediction.

**F2. The same group extended spin chemistry to microtubules themselves.** Zadeh-Haghighi & Simon report a radical-pair model reproducing observed magnetic-field effects on microtubule density, predict additional effects at slightly higher fields, and predict that zinc's effect on microtubule density should show isotope dependence ([Sci Rep 12:6109, Abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-10068-4)).
*Differs from record:* nothing in `index` covers ROS/spin-chemistry routes by which microtubule-directed interventions could shift anesthetic sensitivity. ORCH-C012's verdict is "mixed" largely because of ORCH-E013 (stabilizer and destabilizer moving sensitivity the same way). *My inference:* a ROS-mediated route is at least a candidate for why chemically opposite microtubule drugs share a direction — both perturb ROS handling and cytoskeletal redox environment — and it predicts field- and isotope-dependence that pure cell-biological "general disruption" does not. That is a testable rival to the "general cellular disruption" reading the article currently offers.

**F3. ORCH-C031 is not the only non-interferometric constraint, and one of them is not a radiation measurement at all.** Helou, Slagmolen, McClelland & Chen bound the Diósi–Penrose regularization scale to σ_DP ≥ 40.1 ± 0.5 fm from LISA Pathfinder's measured relative acceleration noise of 5.2 ± 0.1 fm s⁻²/√Hz, noting that 40 fm exceeds the size of any nucleus while the regularization scale had been proposed to be nuclear-sized ([arXiv:1606.03637, abstract; publ. PRD 95, 084054](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03637)). Separately, the Majorana Demonstrator set CSL limits 40–100× better than comparable searches in a 37.5 kg-y enriched-germanium exposure and states that, interpreted in DP terms, its lower bound improves on the previous best by almost an order of magnitude ([arXiv:2202.01343, abstract; publ. PRL 129, 080401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01343)).
*Differs from record:* ORCH-C031/E017 rest on Donadi 2021 (Gran Sasso) alone; ORCH-E019 (McQueen) escapes it by noting Penrose's criterion predicts no radiation. LISA Pathfinder constrains the *mechanical noise* channel, so the radiation-specific escape does not reach it; and Majorana is a genuinely independent underground search of the same radiation channel. *My inference:* the correct statement is that the parameter-free DP model is now squeezed from two distinct observables, and the McQueen escape is narrower than the record implies — it requires denying momentum diffusion as well as radiation, i.e. denying that the collapse has any dynamics that injects energy into matter.

**F4. Diósi's own 2022 primary paper both strengthens and weakens that escape, and it is a better source for ORCH-C030's content than any secondary.** He states the shared claim as collapse at rate 1/τ = E_Δ/ℏ with "an unspecified numeric constant"; reports that the Tilloy–Diósi semiclassical construction reproduces that exact rate and fixes the constant at 1/2; warns that the apparent parameter-independence of the gravity-related rate "may be illusory since the rate diverges for point-like mass distributions," adding that Penrose avoids the problem differently; and closes that he and Penrose agree on the ~ℏ/E_Δ lifetime but disagree on how collapse happens, with energy- and momentum-conserving collapse dynamics named as the hard open task ([arXiv:2111.04604, §IV.A and §V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04604)).
*Differs from record:* the edition treats the rate relation as Penrose's and the stochastic master equation as the tested surrogate. Diósi says there exists at least one dynamical realization that lands on Penrose's rate exactly — which undercuts the sharpest version of "the tested model was never Penrose's" — while simultaneously conceding that the regularization the experiments bound may be unavoidable and that no energy-conserving dynamics exists yet. *My inference:* ORCH-C030 should remain unresolved, but the edition's "untested vague theory" framing can be made precise and less rhetorical: the missing object is not a number, it is an energy-conserving dynamics, and its absence is conceded by a co-originator.

**F5. The 2026 tryptophan-network theory literature reports entanglement measures, but its lifetimes are still radiative, not decoherence, lifetimes.** Gassab, Pusuluk & Craddock build a Lindblad master equation from the non-Hermitian radiative generator with explicit 1JFF-derived geometry, evaluate L₁ coherence, correlated coherence and logarithmic negativity, and find superradiant modes exporting correlations fast while subradiant modes retain them; they state explicitly that the reported lifetimes are defined within a Markovian radiative-loss model, and that static energetic and structural disorder localizes the superradiant component and suppresses long-range coherence. They also disclaim the inference of interest: their comparison "does not imply that microtubules must use quantum correlations for neural processing" ([arXiv:2602.02868 — abstract; §"Lifetimes of Subradiant and Superradiant States"; §IV Discussion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02868)).
*Differs from record:* ORCH-C010 is "unresolved — unmeasured," and the article warns that radiative lifetime ≠ dephasing time. This is a 2026 instance of exactly that hazard, in a paper whose observables (logarithmic negativity) look like the coherence witnesses ORCH-R001 asks for. It adds to ORCH-E035/E036: still no thermal/solvent bath, still no 310 K dephasing estimate, and now a disorder result that runs *against* the proponent claim that collectivity strengthens with assembly size.

**F6. There is a new, partly independent experiment on the superradiance observable, and it shows the effect size is scattering-limited.** Gassab & Craddock measure porcine tubulin dimers and taxol-stabilized microtubules in microplate geometry: polymerization raises quantum yield at 280 nm, but because microtubule absorbance must be treated for wavelength-dependent scattering, the polymerization change is reported only as a bound, ranging from about +6.1% (QY_raw) to about +46.8% (QY_bgA); added free L-tryptophan reproducibly quenches microtubule emission at both wavelengths; and in MD-derived ensembles the section is headed "Structural variability suppresses superradiance," with maximal superradiant enhancement dropping substantially relative to the ordered reference ([arXiv:2604.18604 — abstract; Results §"Structural variability suppresses superradiance"; Results §"Polymerization increases yield at 280 nm"](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18604)).
*Differs from record:* ORCH-C021 is "provisionally supported" on the direction of the quantum-yield trend (ORCH-E004). The direction survives here in a different lab with porcine tubulin — but the magnitude spans nearly an order of magnitude depending on a scattering correction, and the ordered-lattice enhancement that motivates the superradiance reading is degraded by realistic structural disorder. The L-tryptophan quench is a second instance of the ORCH-E003 problem: the optical observable moves when you add an ordinary small molecule that is not an anesthetic.

**F7. The newest proponent statement overclaims on coherence and omits the contradicting animal study.** In a 2025 Medical Gas Research commentary, Wiest writes that we now have "definitive biophysical evidence that MTs support quantum optical effects at room temperature," citing Babcock 2024; asserts direct evidence that targeting microtubules can be *sufficient* for general anesthesia in tadpoles; argues that isoflurane's synaptic effects are mediated by mitochondria and syntaxin 1A rather than microtubules, so a classical account of microtubule involvement is unsupported; and holds that quantum-chemical modelling reproduces Meyer–Overton for tubulin in a way "[that] cannot be said for any other candidate molecular target" ([Med Gas Res 16(2):182, body text and Figure 1 legend](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12413878/)). Searching the full text, the strings "epothilone D", "vinblastine", "Li N" and "more sensitive" do not occur.
*Differs from record:* ORCH-E028/E029 already register an independence problem in the affirmative synthesis. This adds a dated, specific instance: the same author group that cited the contradictory Li 2025 result in its own mouse paper's abstract omitted it from a 2025 commentary addressed to an anesthesiology-adjacent readership. *Caveat I insist on:* this is a short opinion piece, not a review, and omission there is weaker evidence of selective citation than it would be in a review. *My inference:* the pattern ORCH-E035 describes — new photophysics absorbed rather than scored — is visible here in one step: a quantum-yield measurement plus a non-Hermitian model becomes "definitive evidence" for room-temperature quantum optical effects, with the dephasing question not raised.

## 2. Best account

The case's rung separation still holds, and this pass did not disturb the shape of it. What it changes is the texture of three rungs.

On the microtubule physics, the direction of the collective-optics result now has partial cross-lab support (F6), which strengthens ORCH-C013 slightly — but it strengthens it as *condensed-matter physics of an ordered aromatic lattice*, which is what the adopted assessment already says. What newly matters is that the two 2026 papers closest to Orch OR's carrier both push against the scaling argument proponents rely on: realistic structural and energetic disorder suppresses long-range coherence and shrinks superradiant enhancement (F5, F6). The proponent claim that quantum effects "get stronger as the MTs are joined into larger structures" (F7) is therefore not what the most recent modelling from an Orch-OR-adjacent group reports once MD-derived disorder is included. And the central absence is untouched: the newest theoretical lifetimes are radiative lifetimes in a Markovian radiative-loss model with no solvent or ionic bath, so ORCH-C010 remains unmeasured in exactly the sense the edition states. If anything, the gap is now better characterized: the theory community has moved to *entanglement-flavoured observables* without moving to a thermal environment, which is where the falsification risk lives.

On anesthesia, the interesting development is not a new animal experiment but a new competitor. The edition frames the fight as microtubule-quantum versus classical multi-target pharmacology, with the xenon isotope result as an awkward datum that "says nothing whatsoever about microtubules." The radical-pair literature (F1, F2) converts that awkwardness into a rival hypothesis with its own mechanism, its own molecular carrier (a ROS/tryptophan radical pair), its own quantitative fit to the four published isotope potencies, and — decisively — its own field-strength prediction. The three-way space is: (a) classical multi-target pharmacology, with the β3(N265M) causal evidence behind it for intravenous agents; (b) spin-chemistry quantum biology, non-collapse, non-computational, agnostic about consciousness; (c) Orch OR. Hypothesis (b) explains the one datum that embarrasses (a) without incurring any of (c)'s costs — no millisecond coherence, no collapse, no gravitational physics. It also has a striking convergence with (c)'s own literature: the 2026 Lindblad paper names redox/ROS chemistry on aromatic residues as a plausible in-cell source of the excitations it models (F5), and the xenon radical-pair model's proposed partner radical is a tryptophan cation (F1). The same chromophore, two incompatible physics.

On the collapse physics, the record gets firmer in both directions. The exclusion of the natural parameter-free DP model no longer rests on a single underground radiation search: a second underground experiment tightened the same channel, and LISA Pathfinder constrains a different observable entirely (F3). At the same time, the co-originator's own account (F4) says the shared rate relation carries an undetermined constant, that its parameter-freeness may be illusory because the rate diverges for point masses, that a semiclassical construction does reproduce the rate with the constant fixed, and that an energy-conserving collapse dynamics does not yet exist. The honest reading is narrower than either side's public version: the *rate conjecture* is untested rather than refuted, every *dynamics* proposed for it so far either is bounded by experiment or fails to conserve energy, and the escape route through under-specification costs more than the edition's one-line verdict conveys — it requires denying momentum diffusion as well as photon emission.

Nothing here moves the headline verdict. ORCH-C001 remains a conjunction whose weakest link is an absence, and no record added in this pass removes an absence. What the pass does is make the anesthesia arm's diagnosticity worse for Orch OR specifically (a quantum rival now competes for its best anomaly) while making the physics arm's bound more robust and its remaining escape route more precisely priced.

## 4. Narrative

Three concrete improvements to the short article, in descending order of value.

1. **Add the third option to the anesthesia section.** The article's strongest structural move is separating five propositions; its weakest is that the xenon paragraph ends in a shrug ("any spin-sensitive molecular target anywhere would produce it"). That shrug now has a name, a mechanism, a fitted model of the four published potencies, and a field-strength prediction (F1). Two or three sentences converting the shrug into a named rival would make the "not a forced binary" commitment visible at the place readers most often collapse it. It also improves the conventional-account section, because ORCH-C004 as written ("without any quantum-coherent contribution") is not the only non-Orch-OR position available.
2. **Put a number on the superradiance paragraph's uncertainty.** The article currently reports the quantum-yield trend as rising with hierarchical organization. The cross-lab result (F6) supports the direction and simultaneously shows the magnitude is bounded from about +6% to about +47% depending on how scattering is handled, and that realistic disorder suppresses the superradiant enhancement. That is a more honest version of "measurements now say microtubules are optically interesting" — and it sets up the crux better, because it shows the observable is not yet metrologically stable, let alone anesthetic-potency-calibrated.
3. **Tighten the mountain section.** Replace "escaping an experimental bound by being less specified" with the precise version: a second underground search tightened the same channel, LISA Pathfinder constrains a different observable, and the co-originator himself says the rate's parameter-freeness may be illusory and that no energy-conserving collapse dynamics exists (F3, F4). This is stronger *and* fairer to Penrose, because it names what is actually missing rather than implying evasion.

**Objects and plates.** Both existing plates now do more work than the edition uses them for. **IMG-ORCHOR-P01** (the 1JFF-derived αβ heterodimer) is the exact structure from which the 2026 Lindblad lattice is built — its caption could carry the point that the tryptophan network being modelled lives on this dimer and that 1JFF is also the "ordered reference" against which MD disorder degrades superradiance. **IMG-ORCHOR-P02** (lattice, seam, taxol and peloruside sites) is now doubly apt: taxol-stabilized microtubules are the actual sample in the new quantum-yield experiment, and taxane-class stabilization is the intervention at issue in ORCH-C023/C024. No new plate is needed for this pass; if one were commissioned, the informative one would be a plot-style figure of the bounded polymerization effect (the two scattering treatments side by side), because the case's recurring lesson is that the headline numbers in this literature are model-dependent quantities.

## 5. Next questions

**Decisive now.**
1. *Has anyone measured anesthetic potency under a controlled magnetic field?* This is the cheapest decisive experiment in the whole case. Smith et al. specify it: xenon isotopes of differing nuclear spin, potency measured at fields spanning geomagnetic to a few mT. A positive field dependence supports spin chemistry and is orthogonal to Orch OR; a null result at fields well above 2.5 mT falsifies the published radical-pair fit and returns the xenon anomaly to unexplained. My searches did not locate such an in vivo experiment; that is an unresolved retrieval question, not a finding of absence.
2. *Does any dynamical completion that reproduces τ ≈ ℏ/E_Δ avoid the bounded channels?* Specifically: does the Tilloy–Diósi least-decoherence construction predict spontaneous radiation and momentum diffusion at levels the Gran Sasso/Majorana/LISA Pathfinder bounds reach? Diósi fixes the constant and reproduces the rate; whether that model is already excluded is the sharpest open question for ORCH-C030, and it is answerable from the literature rather than by new experiment.
3. *Does a thermal-bath version of the 2026 Lindblad model exist or can it be demanded?* The prespecified-witness experiment (ORCH-R001) is expensive; the prior question is whether anyone will publish a dephasing-time prediction at 310 K with solvent and ionic fluctuations for the same 1JFF geometry. That number, published in advance, is the pass/fail threshold ORCH-S001 found missing — and the same group now has the machinery to produce it.
4. *Is the polymerization quantum-yield effect robust to geometry?* The +6% to +47% spread is a scattering artifact question, answerable with cuvette or integrating-sphere measurements rather than microplates. Until it is closed, an anesthetic-potency series against this observable (ORCH-R002/R009) risks measuring the correction rather than the physics.

**Access and verification needs.** The Kerskens–Pérez reply (doi 10.1088/2399-6528/acc636) — publisher domain unavailable to me; the ledger's ORCH-C026 pair is currently comment-without-reply. Turin 2014 PNAS — needed in full, since an in vivo spin measurement under anesthesia is more diagnostic than any model fit. arXiv:2512.15393 — fetch failed. The Majorana DP numeric bound — verify in the body rather than the abstract before it is relied on.

**Worth keeping in the deeper record even if the article stays unchanged.** The Wiest 2025 commentary, for the Meyer–Overton-from-tubulin argument (the proponents' most serious unanswered point, and the direct motivation for ORCH-R009) and as a dated instance of the citation pattern. The 2026 Lindblad paper's own disclaimer, which is the cleanest proponent-side-adjacent statement in the record that these results do not imply quantum processing. And Diósi's closing remarks, which are the best short primary statement of what the physics conjunct still lacks.
