# Working report — TIK-001 "The Religion with No Name" (pass of 2026-09, bounded)

## 1. Findings

**F1. Ergot in a vessel is a low-diagnosticity signal: archaeochemists use ergot lipids as a generic *cereal* marker.** I opened [Lucejko et al. 2018, *Scientific Reports* 8:3935](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-22140-z) (Abstract; Results, paragraph beginning "Out of the 32 vessels analysed"). *Source states:* the authors present ergot-derived lipids (ricinoleic-acid triglycerides and estolides) as a biomarker for the *former presence of cereals* in vessels, describing *Claviceps* as "common cereal pests"; of 32 vessels from Sardinia, Calabria and Sicily, ten (mostly storage dolia) carried the ergot signature, which they read as evidence of cereal storage/processing, not of drug use. *My inference:* the finding of ergot fragments at Mas Castellar (TIK-C004, TIK-E007) sits inside a documented background rate for cereal vessels; the diagnostic weight of the Pontós find therefore depends entirely on features that distinguish it from ordinary contamination (processing evidence, deliberate concentration, contrast with site-wide grain), none of which the ledger currently verifies at the primary source. This adds a base-rate datum to TIK-R008 and argues that TIK-E007's "moderate" strength is generous. It also identifies the method (ricinoleic-acid/estolide profiling) that TIK-R001 and TIK-R004 would actually use — while noting that it detects *ergot presence*, not alkaloid processing; LSA/iso-LSA or their degradation products would need separate targets.

**F2. The Pontós find has more reported detail — and one reported negative control — than the record carries; all still secondary.** I opened Giorgio Samorini's survey page [*Archeologia dell'ergot*](https://samorini.it/archeologia/varie/archeologia-ergot/) and his [*Ipotesi sugli inebrianti eleusini*](https://samorini.it/archeologia/europa/misteri-eleusini/inebrianti-eleusini/). *Source states (Samorini, citing Juan-Tresserras 2000 and 2002 and Pons 1997):* ergot sclerotium fragments were found in a vessel together with beer residues and yeast, and in the dental calculus of a mandible of a 25-year-old man; the cult place is dated 4th–2nd c. BCE; and ergot was *not* found in the flour residues from the site's mills, which Samorini reads as pointing to deliberate addition. He gives exact locators: Juan-Tresserras 2002, in Pons (ed.), *Mas Castellar de Pontós*, pp. 548–556 (residues/rites) — the same pagination the 2026 paper gives, plus pp. 477–481 for the second chapter — and Juan-Tresserras 2000, *Complutum* 11: 261–274. *My inference:* the mill-flour negative is the single most important interpretive fact about Pontós and it is presently invisible in the ledger; if it holds at the primary source, it is what lifts the find above the F1 base rate. It is also the kind of claim that hardens in transmission, so it must be checked against Juan-Tresserras's own pages before being relied on. Adds locators to SRC-JUAN-TRESSERRAS-2002 (still unverified) and a concrete target for TIK-R004.

**F3. Pontós is not the only claimed ergot-in-ritual-context find; the case summary overstates uniqueness.** Same Samorini page. *Source states:* ricinoleic acid plus ergosterol in two pyxides from the Middle Bronze Age cult area at Portorusso (Otranto), interpreted by the analysts as *Claviceps*; ricinoleic acid in a very small (≤6 cm) vessel from a La Tène-period burial at Czernikowice, Poland; ergot sclerotia in the gut of Grauballe Man, where a quantitative estimate later excluded a deliberate dose. *My inference:* Pontós remains the only find combining ingestion evidence with a Demeter-linked context, but the summary's "the one ergot-in-cult-context find" should be softened. These finds cut both ways: they widen the base rate (F1) as much as they widen the "ritual ergot" pattern. I did not open the primary reports (Aprile & Tiberi 2016; Łaciak et al. 2023; Stolarczyk et al. 2020); they are leads for TIK-R008.

**F4. Bremmer (2014) argues the kykeon was probably *not* drunk at the climax of the Greater Mysteries — which corrects the article and a plate caption.** I opened the open-access De Gruyter text of [Bremmer, *Initiation into the Mysteries of the Ancient World* (2014)](https://scispace.com/pdf/initiation-into-the-mysteries-of-the-ancient-world-59lo0nkd1g.pdf), Ch. I §1, p. 3. *Source states:* the initiates' password preserved by Clement (*Protr.* 2.21.2) — fasting, drinking the kykeon, handling the hamper and basket — describes acts that "could not have been part of the actual Mysteries" because the festival programme left no time for thousands of initiates to fast meaningfully or perform them; Bremmer places them at the Lesser Mysteries or at some other time. Ch. I §3 (pp. 13–14): Plutarch fr. 178's sequence of terror (shuddering, sweating, amazement) *before* the climax is read as staged tension-building by the clergy, a technique shared across Greek initiations. Ch. I §4: quoting Burkert, no mention of immortality, soul, or deification at Eleusis; the performance points to agricultural fertility. Ch. VII (p. 165): pagans noticed similarities to Christianity, Christians stressed differences; Christianity was open, not esoteric. *My inference:* (a) IMG-TIK-P05's caption ("initiates drank the kykeon at the climax of the Mysteries") and the article's framing state as fact what a leading specialist denies; the timing is a live scholarly disagreement (Gainsford, F5, puts it at the end of the fasting day) and belongs in TIK-R006. (b) If the kykeon was a preparatory drink taken days or months before the Telesterion night, the hypothesis needs either a very long-acting agent or a second, unattested administration — a cost the record does not yet price. (c) The "immortality" framing of the book's title is itself contested at Eleusis. This is the primary text behind the Bremmer quotations the ledger carries only via SRC-REASON-2025 (TIK-E018).

**F5. Two caveats: it is a blog post (with exact locators) by a classicist, not a peer-reviewed article; and its chemistry objection (that making *LSD* needs diethylamine) is aimed at a straw man — the live hypothesis is LSA via alkaline hydrolysis, which the 2026 paper addresses (TIK-E008).

## 2. Best account

The Greek half now has two independent pressures the record did not previously carry. First, from chemistry: ergot residues in vessels are so common that residue chemists treat them as a *proxy for cereal* (F1), so the Pontós find's evidential value is not "ergot was present" but "ergot was present *where it should not have been*." That hinges on a reported negative control — ergot absent from the site's mill flours (F2) — which exists only in secondary retellings until someone opens Juan-Tresserras's pages 548–556. If the negative holds and the vessel context is what the excavators say, Pontós is a genuine, single, Iberian datum of possible deliberate ergot use in a Demeter-linked setting. If it does not, Pontós is a barley-beer cup with the fungus barley beers carry. Either way, other claimed ergot-in-ritual finds (F3) show that the pattern is neither unique nor self-interpreting.

Second, from philology: the specialists who have written most carefully about the rite disagree with the article's staging. Bremmer doubts the kykeon was drunk inside the Greater Mysteries at all (F4); Gainsford places it at the end of the fasting day as a restorative and argues the "beatific vision" the hypothesis is built to explain is a Kerényi-era construction from a mistranslated Plato passage (F5). Both readings leave a drug-free generator (darkness, staged terror, sudden light, the *deiknumena*) doing the work — Plutarch fr. 178's terror-then-light sequence is treated by Bremmer as stagecraft. On this account the hypothesis has to pay twice: once for the chemistry (now shown feasible but demanding, TIK-E008) and once for the *occasion* — a pharmacologically active draught taken at the right moment, which the texts do not straightforwardly give it.

The proponents' best reply is not nothing. The 2026 hydrolysis experiment removes the toxicity objection at the level of possibility and was performed by chemists who declare no external funding or competing interest (F6). Pontós, if its negative control holds, is exactly the kind of "ergot where grain isn't" signal that would be expected under the hypothesis. And the absence at Eleusis remains an untested absence (TIK-E009). A mixed reading is available and should be kept visible: a regional or occasional practice of ergot in Demeter-associated drinks (Iberia), with no demonstrated link to the Attic rite; or a rite whose reliable effects were produced by staging, with pharmacology neither needed nor excluded. Nothing found in this pass touches the Christian half except Bremmer's conclusion chapter, which reinforces TIK-E018 from the primary text.
