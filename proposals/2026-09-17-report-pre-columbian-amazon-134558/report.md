## 1. Findings

**F1. A second, larger 1543 narrative of the voyage existed and is lost — and the earliest "invention" charge was aimed at it.**
Opened: Medina's 1894 introduction, [archive.org full text](https://archive.org/stream/descubrimientod00carvgoog/descubrimientod00carvgoog_djvu.txt), §III, pp. xxxiii–xxxv. *Source states:* Medina quotes an autograph letter of secretary Juan de Sámano to Francisco de los Cobos dated 31 May 1543 (Archivo de Simancas, Estado, leg. 61, fol. 208) reporting a man arrived from Peru who "ha navegado por mil ochocientas leguas," and a June 1543 follow-up (fol. 213) saying the "memorial y relación del viaje" was not enclosed because "se traslada y es muy grande." Medina concludes that Orellana presented a long relación to the Consejo de Indias that "hoy no parece en los Archivos," thinks it probable it drew on Carvajal's notes but was distinct from "las dos que conocemos del religioso dominico," and notes that López de Gómara cited it "dándole el calificativo de «mentirosa»." *My inference:* (a) the record's "Invention" account has a sixteenth-century pedigree, but Gómara's target was Orellana's own (lost) relación, not the Carvajal text the ledger reads; (b) Medina himself counts *two* Carvajal redactions (the Relación and Oviedo's), which supports the predecessor's three-witness framing (T'Serclaes / Muñoz copy / Oviedo) for `AMZ-R002`; (c) a lost, larger, self-interested captain's narrative circulating at court in 1543 is a plausible channel for number inflation independent of Carvajal. *Bears on:* `AMZ-C002`, `AMZ-C017`, `AMZ-R002`; adds a locator to `SRC-CARVAJAL-1542`.

**F2. Oviedo's character reference for Carvajal is reputation-based: Oviedo never met him.**
Same text, pp. xii–xiii and n. 16. *Source states:* Medina quotes Carvajal's closing protestation that he wrote "para decirla y notificar la verdad en todo ello… y es verdad en todo lo que yo he escrito y contado," then Oviedo's judgement (citing *Historia general* t. IV, p. 574) that the friar "debe ser creído en virtud de aquellos dos flechazos" and on the strength of "lo que su auctoridad é persona meresce… segund afirman los que le han tractado." Medina's note 16 infers from Oviedo's wish "que hubiera holgado de verle y conoscerle mucho" that Carvajal never passed through Santo Domingo. Medina's own verdict is that the Relación deserves "completa fe," with two caveats — supernatural attributions and enthusiasm for his "Capitán." *My inference:* Oviedo's endorsement is credibility evidence at second hand (wounds + reputation), not independent testimony; the predecessor logged it as credibility evidence, and this sharpens why it should stay separate from diagnosticity. It also confirms that Oviedo's redaction reached him by text and/or through Orellana's men, not from the friar — relevant to how independent the Oviedo witness is for `AMZ-C002`. *Bears on:* `AMZ-C017`, `AMZ-E005`, `AMZ-R002`.

**F3. Confirmed by my own reading: the Muñoz copy is incomplete.** Same text, p. x–xi: "existe una copia incompleta en la colección de documentos reunida por Muñoz que se conserva en la Real Academia de la Historia." This confirms the predecessor's F2 at that locator; nothing new, but it moves that statement from one reader to two.

**F4. The Upano occupation whose signal was "erased" ended by 300–600 CE.**
Opened: ScienceOpen record of Rostain et al. 2024, [Two thousand years of garden urbanism in the Upper Amazon](https://www.scienceopen.com/document?vid=71f8aa2d-382a-4380-a35f-dabcc1dd0335) (abstract only). *Source states:* excavations "date the occupation from around 500 BCE to between 300 and 600 CE," with platforms, plazas, drainages, terraces and a road network "extending over tens of kilometers." *My inference:* `AMZ-C022` (and the article's line "a palaeoecological negative means less the older the activity") rests on a site with roughly a millennium of forest recovery before the Colonial signal. That supports the time-graded-erasure point but does **not** license the implicit analogy to Carvajal's corridors, which were depopulated in the 1600s and have had only ~350–400 years of recovery. The claim's scope should say so. Upano is also a third instance (with Aquiry and Casarabe) of ancient grandeur that is not contact-era evidence. *Bears on:* `AMZ-C022`, `AMZ-C014`, `SRC-BUSH-2025-UPANO`.

**F5. Markham 1859: structure of the volume, and two dated facts for the Ursúa witness.**
Opened: Markham (ed.), *Expeditions into the Valley of the Amazons*, [archive.org full text](https://archive.org/stream/expeditionsintov00markrich/expeditionsintov00markrich_djvu.txt), Contents and Introduction pp. vii–xx. *Source states:* the Orellana narrative in this volume is translated from Herrera's sixth decade (not Carvajal, whose Relación was unpublished until 1894); Acuña 1639 is printed in numbered sections (Markham cites "Acuna, No. 47, p. 91, of this volume"); and the volume closes with a "List of the Principal Tribes… mentioned in the voyages of Orellana and Acuña." Intro pp. viii–ix: Ursúa's expedition left in September 1560, "arrived off a village called Machiparo," and Ursúa was murdered on "new year's day, 1561." Intro p. vii: the Omaguas were the object of El Dorado rumours in Peru. *My inference:* the tribe list is a ready-made 1542-vs-1639 place-name concordance for `AMZ-R001`/`AMZ-R005`; the 1560–61 chronology fixes the Ursúa witnesses at Machiparo eighteen years after Carvajal; and the Omagua–El Dorado expectation is a motive for inflation among the 1560 chroniclers that `AMZ-C003`/`AMZ-E007` should weigh (their convergence on mundane detail remains the counter-argument). The Acuña text itself was **not** reached; the `declined` block on it stands.

## 2. Best account

Nothing in this pass moves the case verdict. It tightens the *credibility* layer around Carvajal in two directions. Against the friar: the 1543 court record shows the voyage generated at least one other, larger narrative — Orellana's own — that a contemporary historian called a lie, and that narrative, not Carvajal's, is where the "invention" charge began. A captain lobbying for a governorship is a more obvious inflation source than a chaplain, and any number that reached print in the sixteenth century may have travelled through him. For the friar: his text ends with an explicit truth-oath, and the one contemporary who vouched for him (Oviedo) did so at second hand — which means Oviedo's version of the Relación is a textual witness, not a personal interview, and its independence from the Medina branch is a matter of transmission, exactly what `AMZ-R002` is meant to sort out.

On the sparse-Amazon side, the Upano dating is a genuine qualifier. The record uses Upano to say palaeoecological negatives weaken with age; that is right, but the demonstrated erasure covers roughly a millennium. The corridors Carvajal saw were emptied only ~400 years before modern coring, so `AMZ-C022` cannot be spent to discount contact-era negatives in the west and interfluves (`AMZ-C014`) to the same degree. Both halves of the mosaic reading survive, but the "time-graded erasure" defence is narrower than the article implies.

The disappearance leg remains the record's thinnest. The 1639 (Acuña), 1647–50 (Laureano), and 1686–1723 (Fritz) corridor witnesses are all digitized and none has been read by this ledger. Until one is, the jump from the 1560 chroniclers to Newson's upper-Amazon ratios (`AMZ-E008`) is bridged by inference, and the modest-skeptic reading — that the later emptiness partly records a modest reality — cannot be tested against the one province (Omagua) where a seventeenth-century resident missionary apparently counted.

- The article's "For most of the twentieth century, educated opinion held that he was lying" could gain one sentence: the charge is older — a 1543 court narrative by Orellana himself was already being called a lie by Gómara — and it was aimed at the captain, not the chaplain (F1). - The Upano sentence in "The sparse Amazon" should state the end-date of the Upano occupation (300–600 CE) so readers see that the erasure demonstrated is over a millennium, not four centuries (F4).
