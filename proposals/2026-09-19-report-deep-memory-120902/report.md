# Working report — DEEP-001 "Deep Memory" (bounded pass, primary literature on the handled objects, the Rapa Nui statue, Oannes, Pillar 43 and Urfa Man)

## 1. Findings

Ordered by how much each moves the ledger. Source statements are marked **(observed)**; my readings are marked **(inference)**.

**F1. The excavators' own record of Pillar 43 has been read, and it uses the word "handle-like".**
[Schmidt, K., "Animals and a Headless Man at Göbekli Tepe", *Neo-Lithics* 2/06, pp. 38–40](http://www.exoriente.org/docs/00066.pdf) (PDF opened; p. 39, col. 2, and Fig. 2). **(observed)** Schmidt describes, above the vulture and "ibis", three big, similarly carved box-like objects with handle-like attachments, each accompanied by an animal, and a line of angles forming the upper limit of the relief. On p. 38 he reports a sculpture of a snarling predator found in situ on Pillar 27 in Enclosure C (Figs. 1a–b), animal and pillar in one piece. He dates both enclosures "about 9000 BC" in the figure captions. **(inference)** This satisfies the truth condition of the declined claim "The top register of Pillar 43 … carries three arched handled forms with small animals. True if the excavators' record of the pillar shows them", which was set aside because it was anchored to the founder's caption rather than the excavators' record. What changed: the excavators' record is now in hand. It also supplies a primary anchor for the `IMG-DEEP-P01` description and partial primary footing for `DEEP-C045` (the "snarling predator" is Schmidt's phrase; the boar/predator distinction as an arrangement is in the blog post below, F2b).

**F2a. The excavation team's proposed reading of the three forms is *buildings*, not containers — and they say no decision is possible.**
[Dietrich, O., "Of animals and a headless man. Göbekli Tepe, Pillar 43", Tepe Telegrams, 14 Oct 2016 (updated 5 Mar 2017)](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/10/14/of-animals-and-a-headless-man-gobekli-tepe-pillar-43/), paragraph beginning "On the uppermost part of Pillar 43". **(observed)** The post describes a row of three rectangular objects with cupola-like 'arches' on top, each with an animal added on the arch; it says their meaning is hard to guess, proposes they might represent the enclosures seen from the side (rectangle = perimeter wall, cupola = roof) with the emblematic animal of each building's group, notes this would be an unusually schematic sectional view compared with the site's naturalistic imagery, and closes: "A final decision on the meaning of these images is not possible at the moment." The same post offers three readings of the disc (egg, sun/moon, or the headless man's head). **(inference)** This directly supports `DEEP-C012` and `DEEP-C066` (function uncertain; no secure identification) and should move both from *unresolved* toward *provisionally supported*. It also adds something the edition lacks: the excavators' own candidate reading is not a handled object at all. If that reading were right the "handbag" comparison loses its Göbekli leg entirely; if wrong, the forms remain unidentified. Either way the Nimrud bucket is being compared with a shape whose diggers do not read as a bucket.

**F2b. The excavator on later Near Eastern parallels: "missing links".**
Same post, comments, reply by Oliver Dietrich dated 17 Oct 2016 to a reader asking about Sumerian/Assyrian bird-gods in similar posture holding a ball. **(observed)** Dietrich says there is evidence that some Göbekli Tepe imagery survives into later seal images (citing Atakuman 2015, *Cambridge Archaeological Journal* 25/4) and that some sculptures recall later objects in gesture and composition, but that at present there are far too many "missing links" to be sure of survival or of meaning change. **(inference)** This is the excavation team's own position on the *contact/inheritance* account for the bird-figure and handled-object comparison: possible local continuity into Anatolian Neolithic seals, no chain to Assyria. It bears on `DEEP-C004`, `DEEP-C026` and the edition's "dated intermediates" test — an intermediate class (stamp seals) is named by the diggers, and should be examined rather than assumed absent.

**F2c. Enclosure animal emblems.** [Dietrich, O., "Emblematic signs? On the iconography of animals at Göbekli Tepe", Tepe Telegrams, 16 Aug 2016](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/08/16/emblematic-signs-on-the-iconography-of-animals-at-gobekli-tepe/), paragraphs 1–2. **(observed)** Snakes prevail in Enclosure A, foxes in B, boars in C, birds in D; the post lists bears, boars, snakes, foxes, wildcats, aurochs, gazelle, quadruped reptiles, birds, spiders, insects and scorpions among the species depicted, and says the iconography is exclusively male. **(inference)** Supports `DEEP-C045` in part (boars are the Enclosure C emblem; the predator is a distinct sculpture, F1) and gives the article's "stone animals" theme its first primary anchor, including the excavators' own term "quadruped reptiles" for what the founder's page calls "lizards".

**F3. The Jiroft wear-trace study says what the caption says — and more than the caption says.**
[Vidale, M. & Micheli, R., "The Function of a Chlorite Hand-Bag of the Halil Rud Civilization as Inferred from Its Wear Traces", *Iranian Journal of Archaeological Studies*, article 1442 (abstract and reference list opened; full text not retrieved)](https://ijas.usb.ac.ir/article_1442.html). **(observed)** The abstract describes wear traces visible on the handle and along the contour of a chlorite "hand-bag" exhibited in the National Archaeological Museum, Tehran; says the strong wear suggests long suspension on a cord during which the object was polished by rubbing against a soft surface, most probably vertical and cloth-covered; states the inference rests on a single object and is "far from being granted"; and reports that the object was later broken and restored with iron fittings and nails, proposing analysis of that iron for meteoritic metals. The reference list includes Muscarella 1993 "Intercultural Style 'Weights'" and Pittman 1990 "Steatite or Chlorite Handles Weights". **(inference)** `DEEP-C009` is supported at abstract level (its truth condition — that the published wear study reports handle wear of that kind — is met) and can move from *unresolved* to *weakly/provisionally supported* pending the full text. Two additions: (i) the wear pattern is read by the authors as hanging *against a wall*, not carrying — a reading that separates this object from a bag held in the hand (bears on `DEEP-C065`); (ii) the specialist literature the authors cite has classed these objects as "weights" — a functional reading not in the ledger at all. The founder's caption "as if hung on a cord" is faithful; the edition's phrasing "hung container" is not what the study says.

**F4. The published study of Hoa Hakananai'a gives the sequence the caption claims, but not the absolute date.**
[Pitts, M., Miles, J., Pagi, H. & Earl, G., "Hoa Hakananai'a: a new study of an Easter Island statue in the British Museum", *Antiquaries Journal* 94 (2014), 291–321, doi:10.1017/S0003581514000201 (abstract opened; body paywalled)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquaries-journal/article/abs/hoa-hakananaia-a-new-study-of-an-easter-island-statue-in-the-british-museum/E27FFA7F4380AEFA9ECD0861613D6AC7). **(observed)** The abstract reports a photogrammetry and RTI survey; that the statue was likely made with a tapering base to stand in the ground, as seen by HMS *Topaze*'s crew in 1868; that a nearby stone was a *pukau*; that two *komari* petroglyphs were subsequently added; that bas-relief carvings succeeding these are interpreted as a single composition narrating the island's birdman myth as recorded in the 19th and early 20th centuries; that 1868 photographs support this; and that the statue now leans slightly from its intended placing. **(inference)** `DEEP-C059` (birdman carvings later than manufacture) is supported as a *relative* sequence: statue → komari → birdman reliefs. `DEEP-C062` (komari) is supported. `DEEP-C058` (c. 1000–1200 CE) is not addressed in the abstract and stays untested here. `DEEP-C060`, `C061`, `C063`, `C064` (two birdmen, tern, paddles, ring and girdle) are not itemised in the abstract; they need the body text or the British Museum object record. Note also that the study reads the reliefs against the birdman myth *as recorded in the 19th–20th centuries* — the Rapa Nui comparison is anchored to ethnohistoric, not prehistoric, text.

**F5. Berossus' Oannes, read in the fragments.**
[Verbrugghe, G. P. & Wickersham, J. M., *Berossos and Manetho, Introduced and Translated* (Ann Arbor, 1996), F1 at p. 44, F3 at p. 48, comparative table at p. 71 (PDF scan opened)](https://www.tertullian.org/rpearse/eusebius_chron/resources/berossos_and_manetho.pdf). **(observed)** F1 (Syncellus, from Alexander Polyhistor): in the first year a monster named Oannes appeared from the "Red Sea" (Persian Gulf); it had the whole body of a fish, another human head beneath the fish's head, human feet joined to the tail, and a human voice; "Its form has been preserved in sculpture to this day"; it spent its days with men, ate nothing, and taught writing, mathematics, city-building, temple-founding, law, land division, planting and harvesting; at the end of each day it went back to the sea and spent the night there, being amphibious; later other similar monsters appeared. F3 (Abydenus/Apollodorus): Oannes "the Annedotos" appears under king Ammenon, with different sources disagreeing on which reign; four more fish-men appear under Daonos and another, Odakon, under Euedorankhos. The p. 71 table sets Oannes beside "UAn" (U-An) in the apkallu column of Tablet W 20030,7. **(inference)** (i) `DEEP-C053` gains real support: Berossus' own description — fish body over a human head and feet, and a form "preserved in sculpture" — is the fish-cloaked figure, and the translators' table aligns Oannes with the first of the seven antediluvian apkallu; this also gives `DEEP-C052` a textual footing via the Uruk apkallu list, though I have not opened that tablet's edition. (ii) Negative evidence for the civiliser template: in the fragments Oannes does *not* arrive once and depart promising to return; he commutes nightly to the sea and is one of a series of seven. The "arrives from the sea, teaches, departs" biography is a compression. That is the mechanism `DEEP-C034` describes, observed in the founding text of the comparison itself, and is directly relevant to the design of `DEEP-R003` (the original passage has a different narrative shape from the lineup summary).

**F6. The Assyrian bucket in the ritual texts — via a secondary transcription only.**
[Samizdat blog, "On the Banduddu, or Bucket" (5 Jul 2015), transcribing Wiggermann 1992, p. 66](https://therealsamizdat.com/2015/07/05/on-the-banduddu-or-bucket/). **(observed, secondhand)** The transcribed passage states that *banduddû* unquestionably denotes the bucket held by many relief figures; that texts prescribe it for the left hand when another object is held in the right; that in rituals it was filled with water; and it quotes an incantation parallel (KAR 91 rev. 1ff. / CT 17 26 64ff.) in which the exorcist takes the bucket, brings water from the mouth of the twin rivers, casts a spell over it and sprinkles it over the man. **(inference)** This is exactly the textual footing `DEEP-C043` and `DEEP-C044` require, but the ledger should not anchor to a blog transcription. I record it as a lead to Wiggermann's primary; not proposed as a source.

**F7. The Delphic omphalos has an ancient Greek attestation as "centre of the earth" — without the eagles.**
[Pausanias, *Description of Greece* 10.16.3 (Jones translation, Theoi Classical Texts Library)](https://www.theoi.com/Text/Pausanias10A.html). **(observed)** What the Delphians call the Omphalos is of white marble and is said by them to be the centre of all the earth; Pindar in an ode supports their view. **(inference)** Gives `DEEP-C018` its first primary point of reference for the "ancient, in the builders' language" corner of the four-way comparison. Note the passage says nothing about Zeus's eagles; the declined claim about the eagles remains unsupported by this source (the eagle story is usually traced to Strabo, which I did not open).

**F8. Urfa Man: the excavators' description.**
[Dietrich, O., "The 'Urfa Man'", Tepe Telegrams, 14 Oct 2016](https://www.dainst.blog/the-tepe-telegrams/2016/10/14/the-urfa-man/). **(observed)** Not from Göbekli Tepe; found during construction at Urfa-Yeni Mahalle/Yeni Yol, broken in four; PPNB date argued from a T-pillar at the site and the material; oldest known statue of a man, slightly over life-size; eyes of black obsidian in deep holes; no mouth; V-shaped necklace; no legs, a conical plug for setting in the ground; "Both hands seem to grab his penis"; no recorded find context. **(inference)** Supports `DEEP-C021`. Corrects `IMG-DEEP-P07`'s description ("V-shaped lines carved on the chest" → the excavators read a necklace) and adds that the gesture is *grasping*, not resting — sharpening the founder's own distinction between a hand at the groin and a hand on the belly (`DEEP-C047`/`C048`).

**F9. The Göbekli Tepe toponym — a tertiary lead that complicates the caption.**
[Wikipedia, "Göbekli Tepe"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6bekli_Tepe), lead paragraph and the paragraph beginning "Before being documented by archaeologists". **(observed, tertiary)** Glosses the Turkish as 'Potbelly Hill' (citing "Symmes 2010"), gives Kurdish Girê Mirazan or Xerabreşkê, 'Wish Hill' (citing "Kosen 2019"), and says the hill was locally considered a sacred place before documentation (citing "Zekîoğlu 2020" and *Hürriyet Daily News* 2018). **(inference)** Not evidence; but it flags two things for `DEEP-C015`/`C016`: the common English gloss is "potbelly", a shape-word, which is consistent with the caption's "describes the mound's shape" but is not "navel of the world"; and the Kurdish name comes with a local sacred-place tradition, which is a different kind of datum from a Neolithic builders' name and should be traced to Kosen 2019 before anything is recorded. The intake decision to set Wikipedia aside stands; I am using it only to name the primaries.

## 2. Best account

The pass confirms the edition's structural judgement — the outlines rhyme, and the objects are on different footings — but it now lets the ledger say *which* footing each is on, from the diggers and editors rather than from captions.

For the **handled objects**, the four legs now read: (1) Nimrud — a bucket named and used in ritual texts (F6, pending the primary); (2) Jiroft — an object whose only published functional study reads its wear as long suspension against a cloth-covered vertical surface, on one specimen, with the authors' own caveat, and which specialists have elsewhere classed as "weights" (F3); (3) Göbekli Tepe — three forms the excavator called "box-like objects with handle-like attachments" (F1) but which the team's published reading takes for side-views of roofed enclosures, with no decision possible (F2a); (4) La Venta — still untested. The strongest version of the convergence account is therefore not "handles recur because people carry things" but sharper: at least two of the four may not be containers at all, so there is no single functional class to have converged *or* been transmitted. The strongest reply on behalf of a connection is Dietrich's own concession (F2b) that some Göbekli imagery may survive into later Anatolian seals — a named class of possible intermediates that nobody in this ledger has yet examined. A mixed account is live: local continuity within Upper Mesopotamia across millennia is a testable proposition of a different kind from a Göbekli–Nimrud–Olmec chain.

For **bird-men and Rapa Nui**, the published study places the birdman reliefs after the komari, after the statue, and reads them against a myth recorded in the 19th–20th centuries (F4). The Rapa Nui leg is thus the youngest and the most ethnohistorically mediated. This does not refute contact; it means the relevant comparison for Hoa Hakananai'a is with Orongo's own petroglyph corpus and with recorded Rapanui narrative, not with Nimrud.

For the **civilisers**, the founding text of the comparison — Berossus — describes a nightly commuter in a series of seven fish-sages, whose form "is preserved in sculpture" (F5). The composite "arrives, teaches, departs, promises return" is not Oannes. That is the clearest instance in the ledger so far of the retelling mechanism operating on a specific original, and it is also the best argument that the V1 lineup measured summaries. The serious counter remains: a compressed template can still track a real shared structure, and only the blind human lineup on original passages (`DEEP-R003`) can tell — this pass has made that experiment easier to design by putting one original passage on the table.

For the **navel**, the Delphi corner now has an ancient Greek attestation in the users' language (F7); the Göbekli corner remains a modern descriptive toponym on every source seen, with a Kurdish name and local sacredness tradition that need their own primary (F9); Cusco and Rapa Nui were not reached. The four-kinds-of-evidence claim (`DEEP-C018`) is strengthened for one corner, not settled.

Nothing found here supports the lost-civilisation account, and two findings (F2a, F5) reduce the specificity of the resemblance it relies on. Nothing found refutes long-distance contact in general; one finding (F2b) names where a Near Eastern intermediate class would have to be looked for. Overall the verdict should stay *unresolved* but the ledger can now carry primary support for several object-level claims and the article can stop saying that none of the primary literature has been read.

- Pittman et al., 1990, "Steatite or Chlorite Handles Weights" — same, earlier.
- Perrot et al., 2005, "L'iconographie des vases et objets en chlorite de Jiroft (Iran)" — iconography of the Jiroft objects including the master-of-animals scheme.
- Thomson et al., 1891, "Te Pito te Henua, or Easter Island" — earliest widely cited record of the island name for `DEEP-R002`.
- Garcilaso de la Vega et al., 1609, "Comentarios Reales de los Incas" — the Cusco "navel" gloss for `DEEP-C018`.
- Strabo et al., 1st c., "Geography" — usual source for the Zeus's-eagles story about the Delphic omphalos, absent from Pausanias 10.16.3.
- Lee et al., 1992, "The Rock Art of Easter Island: Symbols of Power, Prayers to the Gods" — corpus for komari, birdmen and paddles at Orongo (`DEEP-C060`–`C064`).
