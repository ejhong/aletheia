**F2. Pressure alone, with no breath instruction, made most palpated stiff foci undetectable within about two minutes, and none returned at 24 hours (bears on VASO-C039, VASO-C011, VASO-C004, VASO-E014).**
- *Observed.* Abstract: 44 amateur runners (≥50 km/week, lower-leg pain) screened; gastrocnemius MTrPs in 30 (68%); 103 MTrPs identified, "mean 4.7 per person by palpation; 3.4 by ultrasonography." "After the first compression course, 75/103 MTrPs were no longer detectable. 21/103 were visible only on elastography with reduced stiffness, and 7/103 remained both palpable and sonographically visible. At 24-h follow-up, none of the 75 eliminated or 21 elastography-only MTrPs recurred; 5/7 persistent MTrPs were deactivated after a second session." Methods, "MTrP release": thumb compression "applied in 30-s intervals," with "10 s of rest … to allow for blood re-perfusion into the compressed site," up to three rounds, stopping early "if the VAS score was ≤ 3, or the point softened." A search of the full text for "breath" returns nothing: no breathing instruction was given or recorded. Results: the 21 elastography-only points had mean cross-sectional area "significantly reduced to 9 mm2 (p = 0.0003) and could not be palpated"; the seven persistent points averaged 31 mm² and were "7.4 to 11.2 times stiffer than surrounding tissues."
- *Inference.* This is the first objective, imaged record in the ledger of a palpated stiff focus disappearing within minutes and staying gone for a day. It supports VASO-C011 (a change on that timescale is a change of maintained state, whatever the state). It directly undermines VASO-C039 as written — its own truth condition says "false if pressure alone suffices," and here pressure without any breath manipulation sufficed for 73% immediately and 93% at 24 h — and it qualifies VASO-C004/VASO-E014's two-key framing. Two limits keep this from being decisive: breathing was uncontrolled and unrecorded, so subjects under painful pressure may have exhaled slowly on their own (the study is "no breath instruction," not "breath held constant"); and the reassessment was after each compression course, not time-resolved, so nothing here bears on the seconds-scale time-lock (VASO-C073) or the vascular attribution (VASO-C020, VASO-C174).

**F3. Active and latent points differed in size, stiffness and response — and latent points cleared more readily (bears on VASO-C005, VASO-C033, VASO-C075/C076).**
- *Observed.* Results and Table 2: 67 active, 36 latent; active points had higher VAS (8.9 vs 6.1), larger area (28 vs 17 mm², p = 0.014) and greater stiffness ratios (8.5 vs 6.1, p = 0.006). "After the first round of compression, 60% of ATrPs and 86% of LTrPs were no longer detectable sonographically (p = 0.021)."
- *Inference.* Adds an independent lab (Płock/Łódź, not the NIH lineage of SRC-BALLYNS-2011) to the graded stiffness finding on VASO-C005/VASO-C033, in a different muscle and with strain rather than vibration elastography. The active/latent response difference is not the fresh/old axis of VASO-C075–C076 but is the nearest instrumented cousin: the more symptomatic point is harder to switch off. Context, not support, for those two claims.

**F4. The study's own limitations are the missing arms of VASO-R001 (bears on VASO-R001, VASO-R003).**
- *Observed.* Limitations: cine loops "were not prospectively archived"; "Systematic color/power Doppler assessment was not performed"; "The radiologist was blinded to treatment outcomes but not to the initial palpation findings"; imaging plane at follow-up "may have differed slightly." Future directions: "a matched control group assessed at identical time points without compression therapy, optionally a placebo-controlled condition (light tactile pressure at non-MTrP sites), and a comparative arm."
- *Inference.* The keystone experiment can now be specified as an extension of a design already shown feasible: add cine archiving, Doppler over the same ROI, a recorded-and-manipulated breath factor, sham sites and a no-treatment arm. Note also that the authors' rationale for the 10-s rests explicitly invokes reperfusion — the vascular framing is already in the practitioners' vocabulary, unmeasured.

**F5. A small datum for the construct critique (bears on VASO-C030, VASO-C001).**
- *Observed.* Palpation found a mean of 4.7 points per person; ultrasound confirmed 3.4 (Results: "Elastography confirmed the presence of only some of these"). MTrP prevalence was 68% in a symptomatic, self-selected running cohort; runners with MTrPs had lower ankle dorsiflexion (51° vs 63°, p = 0.005).
- *Inference.* Roughly a quarter of palpated points had no imaging correlate. That is compatible with palpation false positives (VASO-C030) or with imaging insensitivity; it is not a population prevalence (VASO-C001) and should not be read as one.

**F6. The community tender-point survey the essay gestured at is locatable, and its design is not a census (bears on VASO-C083–C089, VASO-R010; seen as search snippet only).**
- *Observed (search-result snippet of the PMC abstract, not opened).* [Croft, Schollum & Silman, "Population study of tender point counts and pain as evidence of fibromyalgia," *BMJ* 309:696–699, 1994, PMC2540791](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2540791/): "Two stage cross sectional study with an initial questionnaire about pain to classify those eligible for an examination of tender points"; two general practices in north-west England; stratified random sample; "250 were selected for examination of tender points on the basis of their reported pain complaints; 177 subsequently participated"; outcome "Tender point count (0 to 18) grouped into four categories." I did not see the Results or Conclusions.
- *Inference.* VASO-C083 (the 18-site exam has been applied to community samples) has a locatable primary. But the examined subsample was selected on reported pain, so this paper cannot on its own support "nearly universal" (VASO-C084) or unselected medians (VASO-C085–C086); those figures, if they exist, more plausibly come from Wolfe et al. 1995 (named below, not opened). VASO-R010 should list both, plus Croft et al. 1996 (named below).

## 2. Best account

The pass changes one rung. Before it, the ledger had no objective record of any fast change at a palpated knot; it now has one, from a lab outside the trigger-point construct's founding lineage, and it arrived with no breath component. A blinded radiologist saw roughly three-quarters of palpated stiff foci vanish on strain elastography after at most two minutes of thumb pressure, with none returning next day. For the case's slogan this is the best evidence yet: whatever a knot is, it behaves like a state that can be switched off in minutes, not tissue that must remodel over weeks. Every one of the three accounts on the table predicts this; none is distinguished by it.

Where it does discriminate is the two-key protocol. The claim that pressure alone does not release a knot (VASO-C039) now faces direct contrary evidence — with the caveat, which should be stated wherever the study is cited, that breathing was neither instructed nor recorded. A proponent can reply that painful sustained pressure elicits slow exhalation spontaneously, so the breath key may have been turned unnoticed; that reply is testable (the inhale-hold block of VASO-R016/VASO-C130) and, until tested, keeps the claim unresolved rather than refuted. The more conservative reading is that the exhale is a facilitator or a felt correlate, not a necessary condition.

Nothing in the pass touches which tissue holds the state. The study measured stiffness inside the gastrocnemius belly (fitting the intramuscular pictures, latched-arteriole and guarding, better than the subcutaneous perforator picture, which still owes an account of intramuscular stiff foci); it took no Doppler, so the blood-first ordering remains unfilmed; and it had no sham arm, so expectation and generalised relaxation remain live. The mixed explanation my predecessor favoured — a centrally maintained, stress-sensitive skeletal state that pressure can switch off, in tissue whose circulation is measurably abnormal, causal direction unknown — is unchanged and now has one more objective leg under it. The latch attribution neither gains nor loses; the arteriole record from VASO-S001 still leans against it.

## 5. Next questions

1. **The inhale-hold block is now the decisive cheap test.** Landfald shows pressure without breath instruction suffices; the perforator account predicts pressure during an inhale-and-hold fails (VASO-C130). Repeating Landfald's protocol with breath recorded (capnography or inductance belt) and randomised to free breathing / slow exhale / inhale-hold would settle VASO-C039 in one session per subject.
2. Open Croft 1994 (Results, Conclusions) and Wolfe 1995 to source VASO-C084–C089; record that Croft's examined subsample was pain-selected.
