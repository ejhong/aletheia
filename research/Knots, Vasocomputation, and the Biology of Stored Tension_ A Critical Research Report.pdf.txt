Knots, Vasocomputation, and the Biology of
Stored Tension: A Critical Research Report
Executive summary
The uploaded essay, Knots of Existence and Vasocomputation , proposes an unusually ambitious synthesis:
myofascial “knots” are interpreted as persistent physiological states; vascular smooth-muscle latch states
are proposed as a low-energy storage mechanism; vascular state is then hypothesized to feed back onto
neural computation; and Michael Edward Johnson's “vasocomputation” framework is used to connect this
loop to predictive processing, trauma, contemplative “knots,” and ultimately consciousness. The essay
explicitly acknowledges that this chain begins with established physiology and becomes increasingly
speculative; it also states that its text was generated by Kimi and Fable.  filecite  turn0file0 
The central conclusion of this review is that several lower-level components of the essay are individually
real and scientifically important, but the proposed integration has not yet been demonstrated . In
particular:
Mathematical knot theory is highly developed and genuinely useful in biology , especially for
circular DNA, topoisomerases, viral DNA packing, and knotted proteins. Type-II topoisomerases
actively simplify DNA knotting below thermal-equilibrium levels, and confinement inside viral capsids
generates measurable knot spectra. There is, however, no evidence that a clinical “muscle
knot” is a topological knot in the mathematical sense . The word knot currently connects these
literatures metaphorically, not mechanistically.
Myofascial trigger points have measurable correlates , including altered local electrophysiology,
chemistry, stiffness, and hemodynamics. Hubbard and Berkoff reported spontaneous needle-EMG
activity localized to trigger-point loci; McNulty and colleagues found that mental arithmetic
selectively increased activity at trigger points; Shah and colleagues found acidic and mediator-rich
biochemical environments; and an imaging systematic review concluded that active and latent
trigger points can exhibit quantifiable biomechanical and blood-flow differences. However,
trigger-point identification by palpation has historically shown limited inter-examiner reliability, and
the underlying entity and mechanism remain controversial.
The smooth-muscle “latch” phenomenon is established physiology. Hai and Murphy's kinetic
model accounts for force maintenance with reduced cross-bridge cycling after myosin
dephosphorylation. But treating this contractile state as a literal long-duration information register—
especially one capable of storing semantic predictions, trauma, or “clinging”—is an extrapolation, not
a result of the latch literature.
Bidirectional vascular–neural interaction is more scientifically plausible than it was when
Moore and Cao proposed the hemo-neural hypothesis in 2008. Their hypothesis argued that
hemodynamic variables might participate in information processing rather than merely supporting

The “Michael Johnson” relevant here is Michael Edward Johnson , who describes himself as a

prediction, medium-term memory, and Buddhist tanhā/upādāna . Based on the literature located

program rather than an experimentally validated, peer-reviewed theory . Current 2026 Edge City

results.
A particularly important neighboring literature actually demonstrates something structurally
reminiscent of vasocomputation: Physarum slime mold stores information in the geometry of a
living flow network. Kramar and Alim experimentally showed that nutrient locations become
encoded in a hierarchy of tube diameters, which subsequently biases transport and behavior.
This is one of the strongest empirical precedents for the general idea that the state of a biological
transport network can encode history and influence subsequent behavior . It is not evidence
that mammalian arteries do the same for thoughts.
The uploaded essay's strongest untested claim is therefore not that blood vessels affect nerves—
they do—but that a spatially localized, history-dependent vascular state can store behaviorally
meaningful information and subsequently bias nearby neural computation after the initiating
signal disappears . That claim is experimentally accessible and should be treated as the core
vasocomputation hypothesis.
The essay's proposed “breath-release” mechanism is currently far ahead of the data. Slow breathing
reproducibly alters cardiovascular autonomic indices such as heart-rate variability, but this does not
establish that an exhale rapidly unlatches a specific arteriole at an identified trigger point. The
crucial experiment—simultaneous high-speed measurement of local stiffness, perfusion, respiration,
EMG and subjective release—appears not yet to have been performed.
A major 2026 development deserves particular attention. Mizrachi, Rottem and Rozenkrantz
reported in Nature Human Behaviour that voluntarily directing attention to an experimentally
inflamed site reduced subsequent histamine-induced inflammatory responses in preregistered
within-subject experiments. The study provides unusually strong human evidence for top-down
modulation of peripheral physiology, while its authors explicitly note that the molecular/cellular
mediators were not directly measured and that extrapolation to chronic disease is uncertain.
This is relevant to vasocomputation because it establishes a controllable cognition-to-periphery
channel; it does not establish a vascular latch mechanism.
Overall evidence judgment: the essay is most valuable as a research-generating synthesis . Its lower floors—

Conceptual, historical, and mathematical foundations
Three meanings of “knot” must be kept separate
The most important conceptual discipline for this subject is to avoid silently moving between three different
senses of knot .
A topological knot is, classically, an embedding of a circle into three-dimensional space—usually or
—considered up to ambient isotopy. Two diagrams representing the same tame knot can be
transformed into one another through planar deformation plus the three Reidemeister moves. Knot
invariants assign quantities that remain unchanged under such equivalences; familiar examples include the
fundamental group of the complement, Alexander polynomial and Jones polynomial.
A molecular knot is a physical polymer configuration that can meaningfully be classified using these ideas.
Circular DNA is especially clean mathematically because it is already a closed curve. Protein backbones are
open chains, so a closure convention or another operational definition is required; Taylor's seminal
computational study explicitly confronted this issue when identifying deeply knotted proteins.
A myofascial “knot” or trigger point is a clinical/palpatory construct referring to a localized tender or
mechanically abnormal region. Nothing in the present trigger-point literature demonstrates that tissue
fibers there constitute a nontrivial topological embedding. The uploaded essay's “knot” is therefore not
currently a knot-theoretic object .
This distinction matters because otherwise the rigor of mathematical knot theory can accidentally lend
authority to a physiological hypothesis with which it has, so far, no demonstrated topological connection.

Historical trajectory
The timeline combines several otherwise separate research traditions: mathematical topology, molecular
topology, smooth-muscle physiology, trigger-point research, adaptive biological networks, neurovascular
neuroscience and Johnson's theoretical synthesis.

knot and catenane fractions as much as approximately 80-fold below equilibrium values, demonstrating
active control of a global topological property rather than passive equilibration.

This result is interesting computationally because the enzyme does not merely react to a local chemical
identity: its activity has consequences for a global property of a polymer. Subsequent theoretical work
explored how geometrical selection or kinetic proofreading can permit such topology simplification.
Viral DNA provides another striking experimental case. Arsuaga and colleagues found very high knotting
probability in DNA extracted from tailless P4 bacteriophage capsids; Monte Carlo polymer simulations
reproduced the tendency of confinement to generate highly knotted molecules. Later analysis of the knot
spectrum revealed information about the chiral organization of DNA inside capsids. Here, knot
statistics function as an indirect probe of otherwise inaccessible three-dimensional biological
organization .
This is a legitimate precedent for using topology as an informational observable—but it is categorically
different from calling a tender muscular region a knot.
Proteins show that topology can constrain biological dynamics
Taylor's 2000 Nature study developed an orientation-independent computational method for locating knots
in open protein chains and identified a deep figure-eight knot in acetohydroxy acid isomeroreductase.
Subsequent protein-knot research has examined how topological entanglement affects folding, mechanical
stability, enzymatic architecture and degradation.
Thus real biological knots can influence kinetics and function. The correct inference is modest but
important: topological state can be biologically consequential . It does not imply that all persistent
biological states called “knots” are topological.

Kramar and Alim's 2021 study moves still closer to Johnson's intuition. They observed a local nutrient
stimulus changing tube-wall properties and producing network-wide redistribution of tube diameters. The
altered hierarchy persisted after the immediate stimulus and biased later migration. Their experimental
observations plus model led them to describe the tube-diameter hierarchy as a form of memory.
The architecture is remarkably suggestive:
This is an empirically demonstrated write–store–read loop in a living flow network .
It strengthens the general scientific plausibility of morphology- or conductance-based biological memory.
However, there are decisive differences from mammalian vasculature. Physarum's tubes constitute the
organism's own motile transport body, and their remodeling occurs through a specialized cytoskeletal/flow
system. Mammalian blood vessels are embedded within a highly differentiated organism whose neural,
endothelial, metabolic, endocrine and mechanical controls are much more complex. Therefore, Physarum
should be treated as a proof of principle for biological flow-network memory , not evidence for human
vasocomputation.
Neurovascular signaling is genuinely bidirectional
The classical textbook picture in which neural activity controls blood flow is now known to be only part of
neurovascular biology. Longden and colleagues demonstrated that brain capillary endothelial cells sense
extracellular potassium associated with neural activity and propagate electrical signals upstream to dilate
arterioles—a mechanism by which the vascular network actively detects local neural demand.
Moore and Cao's 2008 hemo-neural hypothesis went a step further, proposing that blood-flow variables
could directly alter neuronal computation through mechanical, thermal, chemical or other pathways.
A particularly relevant advance came in 2024: Salameh and colleagues reported in Science that physiological
blood-pressure pulsations influenced neuronal activity in rat brain tissue and awake animals through
mechanosensitive ion-channel mechanisms, with effects occurring on rapid timescales. This is genuine
evidence for the causal direction
The scientific gap is therefore narrower than “blood cannot possibly compute.” The real unresolved
question is what information vascular variables carry, how long it persists, whether it is locally
addressable, and whether manipulating that stored vascular state changes cognition in an
information-specific way .

Vasocomputation, Michael Edward Johnson, and adjacent authors
Which Michael Johnson?
The vasocomputation author is Michael Edward Johnson . His OpenTheory biography describes his work as
spanning philosophy and neuroscience and lists Principia Qualia (2016), “Neural Annealing” (2019),
“Vasocomputation” (2023), and later AI-consciousness work. He also reports prior involvement with the
Qualia Research Institute.
Searches also encounter unrelated researchers named Michael or Michel Johnson in neurovascular and
biomedical fields. For example, publications by a Michel J. Johnson concern Doppler/vascular-imaging
methodology rather than the vasocomputation hypothesis. These should not be conflated with Michael

Johnson's three central hypotheses
Johnson's Principles of Vasocomputation states three especially concrete proposals.
The Compressive Vasomotion Hypothesis proposes that rhythmic vasomotion could operate as a
changing mechanical compression field that affects nearby neural resonances.
The Vascular Clamp Hypothesis proposes that vascular contraction can temporarily stabilize or “freeze”
nearby neural patterns or plasticity, effectively acting as medium-term memory or a prediction.
The Latched Hyperprior Hypothesis goes further: prolonged vascular smooth-muscle contraction is
proposed to engage latch dynamics and thereby maintain nearby circuitry in a constrained state after the
original initiating conditions have passed. Johnson maps such persistent constraints onto “hyperpriors” in
predictive-processing language and ultimately onto Buddhist concepts of craving or clinging.
The attractive architecture is:
What is experimentally established: neural activity changes vascular state; smooth muscle can maintain
force efficiently; vascular variables can feed back onto neurons.
What is not established: that the latch stores an identifiable prediction, that it maintains a particular
neural representation, or that erasing the vascular state selectively erases or relaxes that representation.

The latch is a mechanism of force maintenance, not yet a memory register
Hai and Murphy's influential model describes smooth-muscle cross-bridge states involving phosphorylation
and dephosphorylation. During sustained contraction, force can remain comparatively high while
phosphorylation and cross-bridge cycling fall, accounting for economical tonic contraction. Reviews of
the latch-bridge hypothesis emphasize both the reality of economical sustained tension and continuing
complexity in the precise molecular interpretation.
Calling this a memory requires an additional operational criterion. In dynamical systems, any hysteretic
state can be said to contain information about history. But biological memory relevant to cognition

The latch physiology directly supports the first criterion in a limited mechanical sense. It does not by itself
establish the latter two at a cognitive level.
This is a central place where the uploaded essay's language becomes too strong. Describing vascular latch
as a “one-bit storage device distributed through every vascularized tissue” is an illuminating engineering
metaphor, but it should not be treated as an experimentally demonstrated storage architecture.

Other key proponents and adjacent research programs
Christopher Moore and Rosalind Cao are indispensable because their hemo-neural hypothesis provides
the most direct conceptual ancestor of Johnson's “read” path. Their 2008 paper explicitly asked whether
blood-flow changes may themselves alter information processing.
Karen Alim and Mirna Kramar provide the strongest experimental example of a living flow-network
architecture functioning as memory. Their Physarum work should be central to any rigorous future
vasocomputation paper, because it supplies a concrete comparator rather than a purely philosophical
analogy.
Atsushi Tero and Toshiyuki Nakagaki established much of the adaptive-flow-network framework showing
how local flow reinforcement can perform globally useful computation without centralized control.
Michael Levin and collaborators demonstrate that non-neural physiological networks can encode
persistent pattern information. Durant et al. showed that transient manipulation of endogenous bioelectric
gradients in planaria can produce altered regenerative anatomies that persist through subsequent

Current status of Johnson's program
As of August 2026, the sources located here indicate that Johnson's vasocomputation theory is primarily
documented through OpenTheory and related talks/projects rather than a peer-reviewed experimental
literature. Current Edge City material describes a “Vasocomputation: The Vascular Imprint of Stress and
Trauma” project associated with Michael Johnson and Alex Rockhill, including efforts to determine whether
practices such as tension/trauma release alter microvascular tension.
That distinction is important. There is a legitimate research program here, but the appropriate scholarly
wording is presently:
vasocomputation is a testable theoretical synthesis built from established vascular
physiology and emerging vascular–neural interaction data, not an experimentally
established theory of cognition.
Critical evidence audit of the “Knots of Existence” article
The uploaded article is unusually useful because it explicitly presents itself as a stack of progressively more
speculative claims. The main scholarly task is therefore not wholesale acceptance or rejection, but
identifying precisely where evidential support changes category.  filecite  turn0file0 
Myofascial trigger points: real observations, unsettled ontology
Hubbard and Berkoff's 1993 needle-EMG study reported spontaneous activity localized to tiny trigger-point
regions but absent in nearby non-tender tissue. McNulty et al. then studied 14 subjects and found that
a mental-arithmetic stressor increased EMG activity specifically at trigger-point sites while nearby control
tissue remained comparatively silent. This is genuinely intriguing evidence that local trigger-point
physiology can be influenced by psychological stress without requiring obvious whole-muscle contraction.
Yet neither experiment identifies the active structure as a blood vessel. The signal was recorded
electrophysiologically and has been interpreted in terms of motor endplates, muscle spindles or other
neuromuscular sources. Therefore, using these studies as evidence for “the brain can modulate the state
of a trigger point” is defensible; using them as evidence for “the brain writes a vascular latch” is not.
Shah et al. provided a second independent type of evidence. Using microdialysis-like sampling, they
reported that active trigger-point sites showed lower pH and increased concentrations of nociceptive/

Their 2008 study involved only nine healthy volunteers, making the result mechanistically interesting but

Imaging also supports objective local differences. Mazza et al.'s systematic review included 33 studies and
concluded that biomechanical and blood-flow parameters can distinguish active/latent trigger points from
healthy tissue, while emphasizing the need for better reproducibility and diagnostic-accuracy research.
Against this, Lucas et al.'s systematic review found that manual trigger-point examination did not have
sufficiently reliable diagnostic performance to recommend it as an objective test, in part because the field
lacks a universally accepted reference standard. Quintner, Bove and Cohen went further, arguing that
the classic trigger-point construct rests on problematic assumptions; Dommerholt and Gerwin
subsequently contested that interpretation.
The uploaded essay therefore overstates one point when it suggests that skeptics do not dispute the
existence of precisely localizable palpable spots and disagree only about mechanism. The reproducibility
and ontological status of the spots themselves are part of the debate.
Fascia and “densification”
Carla Stecco and colleagues have provided substantial anatomical evidence that hyaluronan is associated

“Vascular clamp,” hypoxia and confounding
Any experiment purporting to demonstrate vasocomputational memory must distinguish an information-
bearing vascular effect from ordinary changes in oxygen, glucose, pH, temperature and waste clearance.
Moore and Cao themselves framed multiple possible hemo-neural mechanisms.
For example, if experimentally constricting a vessel suppresses nearby neural activity, that result alone is
not evidence that the vessel is “computing”: reduced perfusion can alter neuronal activity through
straightforward metabolic limitation. A convincing vasocomputation experiment must show structured
modulation beyond nonspecific ischemic suppression , ideally while oxygenation and metabolic variables
are separately measured or clamped.
The 2024 demonstration of rapid mechanosensitive effects is consequently important: it identifies one route
through which vascular mechanical state can alter neuronal behavior without requiring slow nutrient
deprivation.

Open questions
The field would advance most rapidly by answering a small number of well-posed questions rather than
expanding the phenomenological superstructure.
First, what is the physical object being palpated at a trigger point? Existing electrical, biochemical and
imaging signals do not yet converge on a single lesion type.
Second, does a trigger point possess a reproducible fast state transition? Without objective time-
resolved evidence, “release” remains partly dependent on subjective and practitioner reports.
Third, can smooth-muscle latch persist on the durations Johnson's theory requires? Physiological low-
energy force maintenance and cognitive memory lasting days, months or years are radically different
timescale claims.
Fourth, is neural readout of vascular state information-specific? This is the difference between vascular
modulation and vascular computation.
Fifth, what is actually stored? Vessel radius, myosin phosphorylation, endothelial state, pericyte state,
extracellular matrix mechanics, innervation, receptor expression, local metabolites and structural
remodeling can all generate history dependence. Calling all such persistence “the latch” risks collapsing
multiple biological timescales into a single mechanism.
Sixth, what distinguishes an adaptive vascular memory from pathology? A history-dependent state may
be beneficial—local autoregulatory adaptation—or harmful. A viable theory must predict the conditions
under which persistence increases versus decreases future performance.
Seventh, does the traditional contemplative literature contain anatomically falsifiable information?
That question can be studied, but only through preregistered mapping rather than retrospective
resemblance.

The most defensible version of the emerging picture is:

There is now excellent reason to reject an overly passive conception of vasculature. Vascular endothelial
cells sense neural-associated signals; vessels participate actively in neurovascular communication; arterial
mechanics can alter neuronal activity; living flow networks in other organisms can store environmental
information in their geometry; smooth muscle possesses history-dependent contractile dynamics; and
voluntary cognitive states can alter peripheral physiological responses.

Those findings make weak vasocomputation —the proposition that vascular state is a dynamically

They do not yet establish strong vasocomputation —the proposition that vascular smooth-muscle latches

The striking feature of the hypothesis is that this gap is experimentally bridgeable. Unlike many

resistance, smooth-muscle biochemical state, tissue stiffness, perfusion, neural responsiveness and
hysteresis. A negative result at any major causal link would sharply constrain the theory. A positive
demonstration of write → persistence → content-specific readback would, conversely, establish a
genuinely new biological memory mechanism.
That is the scientifically strongest interpretation of the uploaded essay: not as a completed theory of “knots
of existence,” but as a set of unusually concrete hypotheses sitting at the intersection of vascular
physiology, mechanobiology, pain science, distributed biological computation and embodied cognition.
