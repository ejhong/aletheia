## What I did and why

No change of mine was held and the founder's inbox was empty. I first checked the live case "Before Sputnik" (the Palomar transients) for new literature since its 30 September edition. The arXiv listings for POSS-I, Villarroel, and photographic-plate transients show nothing new after 8 September (the newest items are already in the ledger). So I turned to the weakest point on the site that reviewers had flagged. In "Fire From the Sky" (the Younger Dryas impact case), nearly every evidence record was written in August from a commissioned research summary, not from the papers. Reviewers of #466 asked three things: quote the 2026 volcanic study (Nana Yobo et al.) directly; find the proponents' answer on the Greenland platinum's timing; and stop calling the eruption cluster's forcing "measured".

I read the papers behind the two claims the panel splits on (synchrony, YDIH-C010; the comet-debris scenario, YDIH-C012), and those behind the most heavily weighted records. I then rewrote those records from the texts and wrote a new assessment and edition.

## What I found

- **Synchrony is weaker than the case said.** Meltzer et al. 2014 (read in full) says 3 of 29 sites "fall within the temporal window of the YD onset as defined by YDIH proponents", two of them problematic. The case had said "securely dated", which is not quite the paper's claim. Re-running the proponents' own age–depth regressions with dating errors weighted left 2 of 11 sites in the window (new YDIH-E042). Kennett et al. 2015, the proponents' Bayesian model, has a passage on Lake Cuitzeo. There, alternative age models put the layer at about 15,300 or 27,100 years, and the paper set both aside because they were "inconsistent with the local and regional paleoclimatic record" (new YDIH-E043). That is the circularity objection, in the proponents' own text. Their 2024 rebuttal states their standard: dates should be "not inconsistent with synchroneity" (new YDIH-E046). The 2024 correction to Kennett et al. 2015, recorded as unreviewed until now, turns out to concern only the code printed in the Supporting Information.
- **The blind test was designed with the proponents.** Holliday et al. 2016 records that the split-sample design was agreed at the site in 2007 with James and Douglas Kennett, and that James Kennett did some of the analyses. In that test the post-boundary sample held the proxies at two to four orders of magnitude above the rest.
- **The volcanic rival is weaker than the case said.** Nana Yobo et al. 2026, read in full from PubMed Central, finds a volcanic element pattern at the Page-Ladson boundary. But the column's most unradiogenic sample lies *below* the boundary, and similar layers recur at other depths at all three sites (new YDIH-E044). Two of its five authors wrote the Hall's Cave paper (Sun et al. 2020), so the two are one line of work. Its funding (Texas A&M seed grant, Center for the Study of the First Americans and two related funds; no competing interests) is stated on the source. One oddity: its introduction says synchronized records place the Greenland platinum "50 years earlier than the onset of the YD". Every analysis in the ledger places it about 45 years *after*. I recorded this as a limitation, not a finding.
- **The proponents' answer on the platinum's timing** (Sweatman, Powell, West & Young 2024, *Airbursts and Cratering Impacts*, the Edinburgh deposited version) is that the platinum "defines" the onset and that the ice timescale has a ±140-year counting error (new YDIH-E045). The paper does not cite Svensson et al. 2020. Its competing-interests statement says the Comet Research Group paid its publication costs, and I state that beside the source, per the site rules. This is a different paper from the same authors' *Earth-Science Reviews* reply, which I could not reach.
- **Napier 2010**, read in full from the author's arXiv copy, says in so many words that it does not claim the encounter took place. It argues only that a debris encounter was "a reasonably probable event". The claim it supports (YDIH-C012) asserts viability, not occurrence, so my old grade of "unresolved" judged the wrong proposition.

## The judgment

The new draft assessment (2026-11-02-operator-173156) moves two claims and keeps the case verdict:
- YDIH-C010 (synchrony) goes from mixed to weakly supported.
- YDIH-C012 (comet-debris viability) goes from unresolved to provisionally supported, with low confidence and low diagnosticity.
- The case stays at weakly supported.

The last panel judged the case "contradicted" five to five. I have not adopted that. The draft's reason stands, and the new reading does not change it: nothing in the ledger excludes an event, and the decisive tests have not been run. I name the case under `panel:` so the seats can judge the revised ledger.
